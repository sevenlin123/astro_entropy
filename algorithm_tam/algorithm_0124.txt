**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction of Trans-Neptunian Objects from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a sample of known near-infrared spectra of TNOs.
   - Exclude rare or outlier spectral types to focus on dominant taxonomic classes.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the collected spectra to derive principal components, capturing the most significant variations in the spectral data.
   - Use these components to define a low-dimensional latent space.

3. **Latent Space Definition**:
   - Employ Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to create a continuous probability distribution, forming the prior in the Bayesian framework.

4. **Model Training**:
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometric inputs to latent coefficients.
   - Augment the training data using forward modeling with the target instrument's filter set to simulate realistic photometric measurements.

5. **Reconstruction Process**:
   - For new photometric observations, use the trained AutoML model to predict latent coefficients.
   - Convert these coefficients back to the full spectrum using the inverse PCA transformation.

6. **Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Calculate uncertainties in the latent space and propagate them through the reconstruction to determine credible intervals for the reconstructed spectra.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Use a secondary classifier operating in the latent space to assign probability distributions over taxonomic categories, aiding in identifying spectral types.

8. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Utilize a one-class SVM trained on the latent manifold to detect unusual spectra that deviate significantly from the norm.

This structured approach efficiently bridges sparse photometric data with detailed spectral reconstruction, enabling taxonomic classification and outlier detection in large-scale surveys.

### Core Computational Algorithm for Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Objective:**  
Reconstruct high-resolution spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric measurements using a probabilistic latent-space framework.

**Steps:**

1. **Data Preparation**  
   - Collect a training set of TNO spectra (reference spectra) and their corresponding sparse photometric measurements.  
   - Exclude rare spectral types and outliers to focus on dominant taxonomic classes.  

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**  
   - Apply PCA to the training set of spectra to extract principal components (latent space).  
   - Determine the optimal number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for classification, 8-10 for high fidelity).  

3. **Density Estimation (KDE)**  
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to model the latent space distribution.  
   - Augment the training set with synthetic spectra to smooth the manifold.  

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**  
   - Forward model the synthetic spectra with the target instrument's filters (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).  
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.  

5. **AutoML Regression Training**  
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) to regress sparse photometry to PCA coefficients.  
   - Use the augmented latent space for regularization and numerical stability.  

6. **Inverse PCA Transform**  
   - Project the predicted PCA coefficients back to the full spectral space to reconstruct the spectrum.  
   - Ensure the reconstructed spectrum adheres to the physical constraints of the training population.  

7. **Taxonomic Classification (Optional)**  
   - Train a secondary classifier on the PCA coefficients to predict spectral types.  
   - Output a probability distribution over taxonomic classes.  

8. **Performance Assessment**  
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation to evaluate RMSE, Pearson correlation, and classification accuracy.  
   - Analyze Fisher Information Matrix to determine photometric band contributions.  

9. **Filter Optimization**  
   - Assess the information content of different photometric filter configurations for optimal spectral reconstruction.  

10. **Outlier Detection**  
    - Identify rare spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models.  

**Output:**  
- Reconstructed high-resolution spectra from sparse photometry.  
- Probabilistic taxonomic classifications.  
- Optimized filter configurations for future surveys.

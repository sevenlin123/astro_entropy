### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Step-by-Step Description:**

1. **Data Preparation:**
   - Collect a sample of reference TNO spectra, focusing on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude rare or unique spectral types to ensure the PCA captures the main variability.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Perform PCA on the reference spectra to reduce them into a set of principal components (latent space).
   - Identify the number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for initial analysis, up to 8-10 for detailed reconstruction) that best capture the spectral variability relevant for classification and reconstruction.

3. **Latent Space Definition:**
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to create a smooth probability distribution in the latent space, forming the prior for the Bayesian framework.
   - Determine the optimal KDE bandwidth through experimentation to balance smoothing and fidelity to the original data.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation:**
   - Forward model the synthetic photometric measurements by convolving the spectra with the instrument's filter response functions (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Introduce Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate real observational conditions.

5. **Model Training:**
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., using Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map the sparse photometric measurements to the latent space coordinates.
   - Augment the training data with synthetic spectra generated from the latent space to improve model stability in low-sample scenarios.

6. **Reconstruction Pipeline:**
   - For new photometric observations, use the trained AutoML regressor to predict the latent space coordinates.
   - Apply the inverse PCA transformation to these coordinates to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - Propagate uncertainties from the photometric measurements through the reconstruction process to generate credible intervals.

7. **Taxonomic Classification:**
   - Train a secondary classifier within the latent space to assign taxonomic labels (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type) based on the principal component coefficients.
   - Use the classifier to predict the taxonomic probabilities for new objects alongside their reconstructed spectra.

8. **Validation and Evaluation:**
   - Conduct leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and coverage.
   - Measure metrics such as RMSE, cosine similarity, coverage probability, feature identification accuracy, and taxonomic classification scores.

9. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection:**
   - Analyze the information content of different photometric filter configurations to optimize future surveys.
   - Detect rare spectral types or outliers by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models from the latent manifold.

This algorithm effectively bridges sparse photometry and detailed spectral reconstruction, enabling probabilistic characterization of TNO surface compositions with quantified uncertainties.

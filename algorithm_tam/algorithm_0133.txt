### Algorithm Steps

1. **Training Data Preparation**  
   - Collect and curate a sample of TNO near-infrared spectra, focusing on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type) while excluding rare outliers.
   - Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the curated spectra to define a low-dimensional latent space. Typically, 4-5 principal components capture key spectral variations for taxonomic classification, while 8-10 components improve spectral reconstruction fidelity.

2. **Density Estimation**  
   - Apply Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to the latent coefficients obtained from the PCA transformation of the training spectra. Adjust the bandwidth to ensure smooth, physically plausible spectral morphologies without over-smoothing or fragmenting the latent manifold.

3. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**  
   - Forward model the synthetic photometric measurements for each spectrum in the latent space using the effective filter curves of the target instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam). Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric observations.

4. **Automated Machine Learning Model Training**  
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) using the synthetic photometry as inputs and the corresponding latent coefficients as targets. This AutoML regressor learns the mapping from sparse photometry to the latent spectral space.

5. **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)**  
   - Validate the model by iteratively excluding each spectrum from the training set, generating synthetic photometry for the remaining spectra, and testing the regressor's ability to predict the latent coefficients of the excluded spectrum.

6. **Spectral Reconstruction**  
   - Use the trained AutoML regressor to predict latent coefficients from new photometric observations. Apply the inverse PCA transformation to these coefficients to reconstruct the full spectral energy distribution (SED).

7. **Taxonomic Classification**  
   - Train a secondary classifier within the latent space to assign probabilistic taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics, Methanol types) based on the principal component coefficients.

8. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**  
   - Assess the information content of different photometric filter combinations by measuring mutual information with the latent spectral coefficients. Identify optimal filter sets for maximizing spectral reconstruction fidelity.
   - Detect outliers by analyzing the deviation of predicted latent coefficients from the established manifold, flagging objects with unusual spectral types for further investigation.

9. **Uncertainty Quantification and Performance Metrics**  
   - Compute normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) between reconstructed and true spectra to evaluate reconstruction fidelity.
   - Assess empirical credible-interval coverage to verify the calibration of uncertainty estimates.
   - Measure taxonomic classification accuracy to ensure reliable spectral type identification.

10. **Applications**  
    - Utilize the framework for large-scale surveys to infer surface compositions and reconstruct spectra of TNOs en masse.
    - Optimize future photometric surveys by identifying the most informative filter configurations.
    - Detect and characterize rare spectral types and peculiar objects using the generative capabilities of the latent manifold.

### Final Algorithm Description

The algorithm is a Bayesian framework that reconstructs high-resolution near-infrared spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric data. It employs Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to create a low-dimensional latent space representing the dominant spectral variations. An automated machine learning (AutoML) regressor maps photometric observations to this latent space, enabling the reconstruction of full spectra through inverse PCA transformation. The framework also includes a taxonomic classifier and tools for survey optimization and outlier detection, ensuring robust performance across various applications in planetary science.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework for Trans-Neptunian Objects

1. **Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of TNOs, specifically focusing on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to ensure the training data represents the dominant population accurately.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Perform PCA on the collected spectra to reduce the dimensionality into a low-dimensional latent space. This latent space captures the main variations in spectral features relevant for taxonomic classification.
   - Use the first 4-5 principal components for classification purposes and 8-10 components for improved spectral reconstruction fidelity.

3. **Density Estimation**:
   - Apply Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to the latent space derived from PCA to create a smooth, continuous probability distribution. This step augments the training data, allowing the model to generalize beyond the limited spectral examples.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - Forward-model the spectra using the NIRCam filter set to generate synthetic photometric measurements. Inject Gaussian noise at a relative reflectance uncertainty of 20% to simulate realistic photometric observations.

5. **AutoML Regression**:
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) using AutoML to regress from sparse photometric measurements to the latent coefficients in the PCA space.
   - This step maps the photometric inputs to the latent space, enabling spectral reconstruction.

6. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Invert the PCA transformation using the predicted latent coefficients from the AutoML model to reconstruct the full high-resolution spectrum.
   - This reconstruction is constrained by the physical structure of the spectral manifold, ensuring smooth and physically plausible outputs.

7. **Classification and Outlier Detection**:
   - Use a secondary AutoML classifier to partition the latent space into taxonomic categories (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).
   - Detect outliers by identifying spectra that deviate significantly from the learned latent manifold distribution.

8. **Validation and Information Content Analysis**:
   - Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and credible interval coverage.
   - Evaluate the information content of NIRCam filters to optimize future survey configurations for TNO spectroscopy.

9. **Applications**:
   - Utilize the framework for survey optimization by selecting the most informative filters.
   - Detect rare spectral types and outliers by leveraging the generative capabilities of the model.

### Key Steps in the Algorithm:
- **Dimensionality Reduction**: PCA reduces the spectral data into a manageable number of principal components, capturing the essential variability for reconstruction.
- **Bayesian Inference**: Combines the likelihood (synthetic photometry) with the prior (smooth latent space) to reconstruct the spectrum.
- **Probabilistic Classifier**: Operates in the latent space to identify taxonomic classes and detect anomalies.

### Inputs:
- Sparse photometric measurements (e.g., from JWST/NIRCam filters).
- Pre-trained PCA basis and latent space model.

### Outputs:
- Reconstructed high-resolution spectrum.
- Probabilistic taxonomic classification.
- Detection of outlier spectra.

### Computational Workflow:
1. **Training Phase**:
   - PCA decomposition of reference spectra.
   - KDE augmentation of latent space.
   - Synthetic photometry generation with noise.
   - AutoML regression and classification model training.

2. **Inference Phase**:
   - Input sparse photometry → AutoML regressor → Latent coefficients.
   - Inverse PCA transform → Reconstructed spectrum.
   - Probabilistic taxonomic classification and outlier detection.

### Implementation Notes:
- Focus on the core reconstruction framework without validation or visualization steps.
- Use AutoML tools for efficient model training and hyperparameter tuning.

This algorithm effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral analysis, enabling robust characterization of TNO surface compositions in large-scale surveys.

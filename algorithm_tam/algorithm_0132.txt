To implement the core reconstruction framework described in the paper, follow these steps:

1. **Prepare the Training Data**: 
   - Collect a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) focusing on the dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude rare or singular spectral types to avoid skewing the PCA.

2. **Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the collected spectra to obtain principal components (PCs). Use these PCs to define a low-dimensional latent space. Typically, 4-5 PCs capture dominant spectral variability for taxonomic classification, while 8-10 improve reconstruction fidelity.

3. **Augment the Latent Space**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to smooth the probability distribution of the latent coefficients derived from the PCA. This creates a continuous manifold that helps stabilize the regression model during training.

4. **Generate Synthetic Photometry**:
   - Forward-model the synthetic photometry by convolving the spectra with the instrument's filter throughput curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam). Inject Gaussian noise at a relative uncertainty level of 20% to simulate realistic photometric measurements.

5. **Train the AutoML Regressor**:
   - Use the synthetic photometry and corresponding latent coefficients to train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoGluon framework). This model maps sparse photometric inputs to latent space coordinates.

6. **Build the Taxonomic Classifier**:
   - Train a secondary AutoML classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic labels. This leverages the PCA-based feature extraction, enhancing classification accuracy.

7. **Implement Bayesian Inference**:
   - Perform Bayesian inference using the trained models. For new photometric observations, predict latent coefficients and project them through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectra. Obtain posterior distributions and credible intervals.

8. **Evaluate Performance**:
   - Conduct leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction fidelity and taxonomic classification accuracy. Measure NMAE for spectral reconstruction and F1-score for classification.

9. **Apply to Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**:
   - Use the framework to evaluate filter configurations for maximizing information content. Detect outliers by identifying photometric measurements inconsistent with the latent manifold.

**Final Output**: A probabilistic spectral reconstruction framework that infers high-resolution spectra from sparse photometry, enabling accurate taxonomic classification and outlier detection for TNOs.

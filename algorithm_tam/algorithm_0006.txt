**Algorithm Description: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction of Trans-Neptunian Objects from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Data Collection and Preprocessing**:
   - Gather high-quality near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs), focusing on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type) while excluding rare or outlier categories.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Perform PCA on the collected spectra to extract principal components (PCs) that represent the major modes of spectral variability within the TNO population. This step reduces the spectral data into a low-dimensional latent space.

3. **Prior Construction using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)**:
   - Construct a probability distribution over the latent space by applying KDE to the PCA coefficients derived from the training spectra. This creates a smooth, continuous manifold that approximates the underlying distribution of valid TNO spectra.

4. **Synthetic Data Generation**:
   - For each point in the latent space (defined by the KDE), generate a synthetic spectrum by applying the inverse PCA transformation.
   - Convolve each synthetic spectrum with the filter transmission curves of the NIRCam instrument to compute the expected photometric magnitudes.
   - Introduce Gaussian noise with a relative reflectance uncertainty of ~20% (simulating typical observational noise) to the synthetic photometric measurements.

5. **Model Training**:
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map the noisy photometric measurements to the latent space coordinates (PCA coefficients).
   - Simultaneously, train a probabilistic classifier (using AutoML) to map the latent space coordinates to the taxonomic classes (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).

6. **Spectral Reconstruction and Classification**:
   - For new observations (sparse photometry), use the trained regressor to predict the latent coordinates.
   - Project these predicted coordinates back into the original spectral space using the inverse PCA transform to obtain the reconstructed spectrum.
   - Apply the trained classifier to the latent coordinates to determine the taxonomic type of the object.

This algorithm efficiently reconstructs full spectra and classifies TNOs from sparse photometric data, leveraging probabilistic frameworks and manifold learning for accurate and robust results.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a reference set of TNO near-IR spectra (e.g., 51 observed spectra).
   - Exclude rare or outlier spectral types to focus on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Perform PCA on the reference spectra to extract principal components (PCs).
   - PCs represent the dominant modes of spectral variability and form the latent space.

3. **Data Augmentation**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the latent coefficients derived from PCA to generate synthetic spectra.
   - This creates a smooth, continuous manifold that approximates the underlying spectral distribution.

4. **Forward Modeling**:
   - Simulate photometric measurements by convolving the augmented spectra with instrument-specific filter responses (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.

5. **Model Training**:
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometric inputs to latent coefficients.
   - The regressor learns the non-linear relationship between photometry and PCA coefficients.

6. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - For new photometric observations, use the trained regressor to predict latent coefficients.
   - Apply the inverse PCA transformation to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - Propagate uncertainties through the model to provide confidence intervals on the reconstructed spectra.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Train a secondary classifier within the latent space to predict taxonomic categories (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organic).
   - Classify new objects based on their latent coefficients, providing probabilistic taxonomic assignments.

8. **Performance Validation**:
   - Use Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction fidelity.
   - Compute metrics such as NRMSE, empirical credible-interval coverage, and classification accuracy.

9. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Evaluate different photometric filter configurations to identify those that maximize information content and minimize reconstruction error.
   - Determine optimal filter sets for efficient TNO surveys.

10. **Outlier Detection**:
    - Identify objects with posterior probabilities significantly lower than the prior density.
    - Flag such outliers for detailed spectroscopic follow-up to uncover new spectral types.

### Output:
- Reconstructed spectra with uncertainties for each object.
- Probabilistic taxonomic classification.
- Optimized photometric filter configurations.
- Detection of rare or unusual spectral types.

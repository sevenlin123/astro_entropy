The computational algorithm for spectral reconstruction of trans-Neptunian objects from sparse photometry involves the following steps:

1. **Data Collection**: Gather a set of near-infrared TNO spectra.
2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Decompose the spectra into principal components to establish a low-dimensional latent space.
3. **Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)**: Augment the latent space with synthetic data, creating a smooth probability distribution.
4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**: Simulate photometric measurements by convolving the augmented spectra with instrument-specific filter curves.
5. **Noise Injection**: Add Gaussian noise to the synthetic photometry to account for observational uncertainties.
6. **Model Training**: Use AutoML to train a regressor mapping noisy photometry to PCA coefficients and optionally a classifier for taxonomic prediction.
7. **Reconstruction**: For new photometry, predict latent coefficients and invert PCA to retrieve the full spectrum.

The algorithm effectively bridges sparse photometry and spectral reconstruction, enabling insights into TNO compositions through Bayesian inference and manifold learning.

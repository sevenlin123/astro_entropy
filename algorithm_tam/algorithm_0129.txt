### Algorithm Description

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect and preprocess reference TNO spectra.
   - Exclude rare spectral types and outliers to focus on dominant taxonomic classes.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Perform PCA on the preprocessed spectra to extract principal components.
   - Use the top 4-5 components for initial reconstruction, and 8-10 for enhanced fidelity.

3. **Synthetic Data Augmentation**:
   - Apply Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA latent space to generate synthetic data, creating a smooth manifold.

4. **Model Training**:
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map photometric inputs to PCA coefficients.
   - Simultaneously train a classifier within the latent space for taxonomic prediction.

5. **Forward Modeling**:
   - Convolve the spectra with target instrument filters (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to compute synthetic photometry.
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.

6. **Reconstruction Process**:
   - For new photometric observations, use the trained AutoML model to predict latent coefficients.
   - Project these coefficients back to full spectra using inverse PCA.

7. **Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Quantify uncertainty in reconstructed spectra by sampling from the posterior distribution of latent coefficients.

8. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Classify spectra into predefined types (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organic) based on latent space projections.

9. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Flag objects with low posterior probabilities as potential outliers for follow-up study.

10. **Survey Optimization**:
    - Evaluate information content of different filter combinations to optimize observing strategies.

11. **Performance Validation**:
    - Use leave-one-out cross-validation to assess reconstruction fidelity and uncertainty calibration.

12. **Applications**:
    - Apply the framework to optimize filter configurations and detect rare spectral types in new observations.

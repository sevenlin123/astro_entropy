### Algorithm Steps:

1. **Preprocessing**:
   - **Data Collection**: Collect reference TNO spectra, focusing on dominant taxonomic types (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organics) while excluding rarer types.
   - **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Apply PCA to the reference spectra to derive principal components. Retain 4–5 components for taxonomic classification and up to 10 for detailed spectral reconstruction.
   - **Synthetic Data Generation**: Use non-parametric Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA scores to generate synthetic latent variables, creating a smooth manifold.
   - **Filter Convolution**: Convolve each synthetic spectrum with the effective filter curves of the target instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to compute synthetic photometry.
   - **Noise Addition**: Add Gaussian noise with a relative uncertainty of 20% to the synthetic photometry to simulate observational noise.

2. **Model Training**:
   - **Training Data Preparation**: Use the noisy synthetic photometry as input features.
   - **Regressor Training**: Train an ensemble of AutoML models (Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees and Neural Networks) to regress from the noisy photometry to the latent PCA coefficients.
   - **Classifier Training**: Simultaneously train a classifier to partition the latent space into discrete taxonomic categories (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics).

3. **Inference**:
   - **Input Processing**: For new photometric observations, pass the observed magnitudes through the trained regressor.
   - **Latent Coefficients Prediction**: Obtain predicted latent coefficients from the regressor.
   - **Spectrum Reconstruction**: Project the latent coefficients through the inverse PCA transformation to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - **Taxonomic Classification**: Use the classifier to predict the taxonomic category probabilities.

This structured approach efficiently reconstructs spectra and classifies TNOs using sparse photometric data, leveraging PCA and AutoML for robust and accurate results.

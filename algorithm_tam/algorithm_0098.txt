### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Training Phase**:
   - **Step 1**: Collect a set of reference TNO spectra (e.g., 51 spectra) and perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to define a low-dimensional latent space.
   - **Step 2**: Augment the latent space using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth probability distribution of possible spectral types.
   - **Step 3**: Generate synthetic photometry by convolving the augmented spectra with the filter responses of the target instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - **Step 4**: Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometric inputs to the latent coefficients.
   - **Step 5**: Train a secondary AutoML classifier to partition the latent space into taxonomic groups (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics).

2. **Inference Phase**:
   - **Step 1**: For new photometric observations, predict the latent coefficients using the trained AutoML regressor.
   - **Step 2**: Project the latent coefficients through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - **Step 3**: Use the trained classifier to determine the taxonomic classification of the object.
   - **Step 4**: Calculate credible intervals for the reconstructed spectrum to assess uncertainties and detect outliers.

3. **Applications**:
   - **Survey Optimization**: Test different filter configurations using the forward model to maximize information content.
   - **Outlier Detection**: Identify objects whose spectra fall outside credible intervals for further study.

4. **Validation**:
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation to assess reconstruction fidelity and calibration of credible intervals.

This algorithm bridges the gap between sparse photometry and detailed spectral reconstruction, enabling statistically rigorous characterization of TNO surface compositions from limited observations.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework for Trans-Neptunian Objects

1. **Input Data**: Sparse photometric measurements of TNOs, along with reference near-infrared spectra of TNOs labeled by spectral type (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).

2. **Training Phase**:
   - **Step 2.1**: Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the reference spectra to derive a low-dimensional latent space representation. Select the optimal number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for classification, 8-10 for detailed reconstruction).
   - **Step 2.2**: Augment the training data by generating synthetic latent coefficients using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth, continuous manifold, ensuring the latent space captures the spectral variability of the training population.
   - **Step 2.3**: Forward model synthetic photometric observations by convolving the augmented spectra with the instrument's filter throughput curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters), adding Gaussian noise (e.g., 20%) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.
   - **Step 2.4**: Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoML regressor) to map the synthetic photometry to the latent coefficients.

3. **Validation Phase**:
   - **Step 3.1**: Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess the reconstruction accuracy and uncertainty quantification. For each held-out spectrum, predict the latent coefficients using the trained regressor and project back to the full spectrum via inverse PCA.
   - **Step 3.2**: Evaluate the empirical credible interval coverage (e.g., ~95%) across wavelengths to ensure the reconstructed spectra are physically plausible.

4. **Inference Phase**:
   - **Step 4.1**: For new photometric observations, use the trained regressor to predict the latent coefficients.
   - **Step 4.2**: Project the predicted latent coefficients through the inverse PCA transform to obtain the reconstructed spectrum.
   - **Step 4.3**: Optionally, classify the spectral type using a secondary classifier trained on the latent manifold.

5. **Applications**:
   - **Survey Optimization**: Test various filter configurations by simulating photometric measurements and evaluating their information content for spectral reconstruction.
   - **Outlier Detection**: Identify spectra with latent coefficients in low-probability regions of the manifold, indicating rare or unusual spectral types.

6. **Output**: For each object, produce the reconstructed spectrum, associated uncertainties, and a probabilistic taxonomic classification (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).

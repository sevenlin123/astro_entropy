### Algorithm for Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction of Trans-Neptunian Objects from Sparse Photometry

**Step 1: Data Preparation**
- Collect a training set of reference TNO spectra.
- Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to focus on dominant taxonomic classes.

**Step 2: Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
- Perform PCA on the training spectra to identify principal components capturing dominant spectral variations.
- Select 4-5 components for taxonomic classification and 8-10 for detailed reconstruction.

**Step 3: Latent Space Definition**
- Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on PCA coefficients to create a continuous probability distribution representing valid spectra.

**Step 4: Synthetic Photometry Generation**
- Forward-model the spectra through JWST/NIRCam filters to simulate photometric observations.
- Inject noise (~20% relative uncertainty) to account for observational uncertainties.

**Step 5: AutoML Model Training**
- Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoML) to regress sparse photometry onto PCA coefficients.
- Augment training data using synthetic spectra derived from the latent manifold to improve model stability.

**Step 6: Bayesian Inference**
- For new photometric observations, predict latent coefficients using the trained AutoML model.
- Apply inverse PCA to reconstruct the full spectrum, constrained by the learned manifold.

**Step 7: Taxonomic Classification**
- Use a secondary classifier trained on latent space to assign taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic) based on predicted coefficients.

**Step 8: Outlier Detection**
- Identify objects with high entropy in latent or taxonomic distributions as potential outliers.

**Step 9: Performance Validation**
- Conduct Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and uncertainty coverage.
- Evaluate credible intervals and classification confidence for robustness.

**Step 10: Application to Survey Optimization**
- Assess various filter configurations for their ability to accurately reconstruct spectra and detect outliers, aiding in planning efficient photometric surveys.

This algorithm systematically bridges sparse photometric data with detailed spectral analysis, enabling efficient and accurate reconstruction of TNO spectra for large-scale surveys.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Data Preparation**:
   - Collect sparse photometric measurements of TNOs.
   - Obtain a reference set of near-infrared spectra for training (e.g., 51 observed spectra).

2. **Latent Space Definition**:
   - Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the reference spectra to extract principal components (PCs).
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to construct a continuous probability distribution in the latent space, augmenting the training data.

3. **Forward Modeling**:
   - Simulate photometric measurements for synthetic spectra by convolving them with the instrument's filter responses (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.

4. **Training the Regressor**:
   - Use AutoML (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to train a model that maps sparse photometry to latent space coordinates.
   - The model is trained on the synthetic data created from the augmented latent manifold.

5. **Taxonomic Classifier**:
   - Train another AutoML model on the latent coordinates to classify TNO spectra into taxonomic groups (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics).

6. **Reconstruction Workflow**:
   - For new photometric observations:
     - Use the trained regressor to predict latent coordinates.
     - Project the latent coordinates back to full spectra using inverse PCA.
     - Classify the spectral type using the taxonomic classifier.

7. **Validation**:
   - Conduct leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and classification performance.
   - Evaluate metrics such as MSE, RMSE, correlation coefficient, and credible interval coverage.

8. **Output**:
   - Reconstructed full spectra and taxonomic classifications for each object.

This algorithm enables the probabilistic reconstruction of detailed spectra from sparse photometric data, bridging the gap between available observations and comprehensive spectroscopic analysis.

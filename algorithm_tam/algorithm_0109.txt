### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**1. Data Preparation:**
- **Input:** Sparse photometric observations of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) across selected filters.
- **Output:** Probabilistic reconstruction of the full near-infrared spectrum.

**2. Latent Space Definition (Prior):**
   - Train Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on a reference set of TNO spectra to extract principal components (latent space).
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to construct a continuous probability distribution over the latent space.

**3. Training the Encoder:**
   - Generate synthetic photometry by forward modeling PCA-derived spectra through JWST/NIRCam filter curves.
   - Introduce noise (20% relative uncertainty) to simulate real-world photometric uncertainties.
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map noisy photometry to latent space coefficients.

**4. Bayesian Inference:**
   - For each object, use the trained encoder to predict latent coefficients from photometry.
   - Apply inverse PCA transformation to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - Propagate photometric uncertainties through the reconstruction to provide confidence intervals.

**5. Taxonomic Classification:**
   - Classify objects by projecting predicted latent coefficients into a pre-trained classifier, which distinguishes between spectral types (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organics, Methanol).

**6. Outlier Detection:**
   - Identify objects whose reconstructed spectra significantly deviate from the latent manifold, indicating rare or unusual spectral types.

**7. Output:**
   - Reconstructed spectrum with uncertainties.
   - Probabilistic taxonomic classification.
   - Detection of spectral outliers for follow-up spectroscopy.

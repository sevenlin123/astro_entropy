**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction of Trans-Neptunian Objects from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) as the training set.
   - Exclude rare spectral types to focus on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Perform PCA on the training spectra to derive a low-dimensional latent space representation.
   - Determine the number of principal components (typically 4-10) that capture the majority of spectral variability relevant for classification and reconstruction.

3. **Latent Space Augmentation**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA-derived latent coefficients to create a smooth probability distribution, forming a continuous manifold.
   - This augmented latent space serves as the prior for spectral reconstruction.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - For each augmented spectrum, simulate photometric measurements by convolving with the instrumental response (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).
   - Introduce noise (e.g., 20% relative reflectance uncertainty) to simulate real-world photometric uncertainties.

5. **AutoML Model Training**:
   - Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) using the synthetic photometry as inputs and the corresponding latent coefficients as outputs.
   - This regressor maps sparse photometric data to the latent space.

6. **Bayesian Inference for Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Given new photometric observations, use the trained AutoML model to predict latent coefficients.
   - Apply the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum, constrained by the learned spectral manifold.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Train a secondary AutoML classifier on the latent space to predict the taxonomic type (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organic) of each object.
   - The classifier operates on the latent coefficients to assign probabilities for each spectral type.

8. **Validation and Applications**:
   - Validate the framework using Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction fidelity, uncertainty calibration, and taxonomic accuracy.
   - Apply the framework to survey optimization by identifying optimal filter configurations for JWST/NIRCam.
   - Detect outliers in the latent space to identify peculiar spectral types requiring further investigation.

This structured approach enables efficient and statistically rigorous mapping of TNO compositional diversity from sparse photometric data, bridging the gap between photometry and spectroscopy.

### Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Algorithm

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Gather a set of reference TNO spectra.
   - Exclude rare or outlier spectral types to focus on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the reference spectra to reduce them into a low-dimensional latent space.
   - Determine the optimal number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for classification, 8-10 for detailed reconstruction) based on explained variance and cross-validation results.

3. **Data Augmentation with Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)**:
   - Project the reference spectra onto the PCA latent space.
   - Use KDE to smooth and interpolate the latent space, generating synthetic spectra to expand the training dataset.
   - Adjust KDE bandwidth to balance local fidelity and global smoothness (e.g., h ≈ 0.5).

4. **Forward Modeling Synthetic Photometry**:
   - Simulate photometric measurements by convolving the spectra with the instrument's filter response curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to account for observational uncertainties.

5. **Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) Regression**:
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map the synthetic photometry to the PCA latent coefficients.
   - Use the AutoML framework to handle hyperparameter tuning and model selection without manual intervention.

6. **Inverse PCA Transformation**:
   - Reconstruct the full spectra from the predicted latent coefficients using the inverse PCA transformation.
   - Ensure the reconstructed spectra inherit the physical smoothness and absorption features of the training population.

7. **Taxonomic Classification in Latent Space**:
   - Train a secondary AutoML classifier on the PCA latent coordinates to predict taxonomic categories (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).
   - Classify new observations by projecting their latent coefficients and predicting their taxonomic probabilities.

8. **Validation and Cross-Validation**:
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to evaluate reconstruction accuracy and credible interval coverage.
   - Assess the model's ability to detect outliers and peculiar spectral types by analyzing deviations in the latent manifold.

9. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**:
   - Simulate different photometric filter configurations to identify those that maximize spectral reconstruction fidelity.
   - Detect outliers by identifying points that deviate significantly from the continuous latent manifold.

10. **Reconstruction and Uncertainty Propagation**:
    - Propagate photometric uncertainties through the Bayesian framework to generate probabilistic reconstructions with credible intervals.
    - Use the reconstructed spectra to measure diagnostic features (e.g., absorption bands, spectral slopes) inaccessible from photometry alone.

This algorithm enables the reconstruction of high-fidelity TNO spectra from sparse photometry, facilitating taxonomic classification, survey optimization, and outlier detection in large-scale datasets.

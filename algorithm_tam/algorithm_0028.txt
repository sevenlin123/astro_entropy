### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework

**Step-by-Step Description:**

1. **Training Data Preparation:**
   - Collect a seed set of reference TNO spectra (approximately 51 spectra) belonging to dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude rare or unusual spectral types to ensure a consistent latent space.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Perform PCA on the training spectra to extract principal components (latent space).
   - Determine the number of components (K) that capture the majority of spectral variability (e.g., 4-10 components).

3. **Latent Space Augmentation:**
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth probability distribution in the latent space.
   - This augments the training data, allowing for interpolation between existing spectra.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation:**
   - For each augmented spectrum, generate synthetic photometry by convolving with instrument-specific filter curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Introduce Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate real photometric measurements.

5. **AutoML Model Training:**
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) to regress from photometric inputs to latent coefficients.
   - Use the synthetic photometry as training data.

6. **Inverse PCA Transform:**
   - Implement the inverse PCA transformation to reconstruct full spectra from predicted latent coefficients.

7. **Bayesian Inference:**
   - Apply Bayesian framework to model the posterior distribution of spectra given photometric observations.
   - Propagate measurement uncertainties to reconstructed spectra.

8. **Taxonomic Classification:**
   - Train a secondary AutoML classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).
   - Use predicted latent coefficients for classification.

9. **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV):**
   - Validate the framework by removing one spectrum at a time and assessing reconstruction accuracy and fidelity.

10. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection:**
    - Use the framework to evaluate different photometric filter configurations for information content.
    - Detect outliers by identifying latent space predictions far from the training manifold.

11. **Output:**
    - For each new observation, provide reconstructed spectrum, taxonomic classification probabilities, and uncertainty estimates.

This algorithm enables reconstructing detailed TNO spectra from sparse photometry, supporting survey optimization and outlier detection in planetary science.

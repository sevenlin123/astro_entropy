**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Training Data Preparation**
   - Collect a sample of near-infrared spectra of TNOs, focusing on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to ensure a representative training set.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
   - Apply PCA to the collected spectra to derive principal components (latent space).
   - Use the eigenvectors as a basis for reconstructing full spectra from reduced representations.

3. **Data Augmentation**
   - Project the seed spectra onto the PCA latent space to obtain latent coefficients.
   - Employ Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to generate synthetic latent vectors, expanding the training data while maintaining physical plausibility.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**
   - Forward model the synthetic spectra through the target instrument's filters (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to create synthetic photometric data.
   - Introduce Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate real-world photometric uncertainties.

5. **AutoML Regression Training**
   - Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoML) to map sparse photometric inputs to latent coefficients.
   - The model learns the mapping from photometry to PCA latent space, capturing the underlying spectral structure.

6. **Posterior Inference for Spectral Reconstruction**
   - For new photometric observations, use the trained AutoML model to predict latent coefficients.
   - Project these coefficients back through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full high-resolution spectrum.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**
   - Use a secondary AutoML classifier trained on latent coefficients to predict the taxonomic type of the object.
   - Classify the object into predefined categories (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type) based on the latent representation.

8. **Output**
   - Provide both the reconstructed spectrum and the probabilistic taxonomic classification, enabling analysis of surface composition and structure from sparse photometric data.

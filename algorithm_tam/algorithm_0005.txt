### Algorithm Description

1. **Data Preparation**:
   - Gather near-infrared spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) as the training dataset.
   - Exclude rare spectral types and outliers to focus on dominant taxonomic groups.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the training spectra to extract principal components (PCs).
   - PCs represent the dominant modes of spectral variability.

3. **Latent Space Definition**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to define a continuous probability distribution in the latent space.
   - KDE ensures smooth interpolation between training examples, creating a manifold of plausible spectral representations.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - Forward model the synthetic photometry for each augmented spectrum by convolving with the instrument's filter responses (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.

5. **Training the AutoML Regressor**:
   - Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees using the synthetic photometry as inputs and PCA coefficients as targets.
   - The regressor learns the mapping from sparse photometry to latent space coordinates.

6. **Taxonomic Classifier Training**:
   - Train a secondary AutoML classifier on the PCA coefficients to predict taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).
   - Classify based on the latent space representation for improved accuracy.

7. **Spectral Reconstruction Pipeline**:
   - For new photometric observations, pass the photometry through the trained regressor to predict latent coefficients.
   - Project the latent coefficients back to the full spectrum using the inverse PCA transform.
   - Obtain a reconstructed spectrum and associated uncertainties.

8. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Compare the distance between observed photometry and reconstructed spectrum.
   - Use posterior probabilities of latent space membership to identify unusual objects.

9. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Evaluate information content of different filter configurations using expected entropy reduction.
   - Select optimal filters (e.g., F090W, F115W, F410M, F460M) for maximizing taxonomic distinction.

10. **Validation and Application**:
    - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation to assess reconstruction fidelity and credible interval coverage.
    - Apply the framework to detect rare spectral types and optimize future photometric surveys.

This structured approach effectively bridges sparse photometry and detailed spectral reconstruction, enabling robust taxonomic classification and outlier detection in TNO studies.

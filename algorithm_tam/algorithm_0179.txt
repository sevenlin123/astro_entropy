### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Training Phase:**
   - **Input:** Reference near-infrared spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
   - **Step 1:** Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the reference spectra to extract principal components (PCs). The number of PCs (latent dimensions) is determined empirically, typically between 4 to 10, based on cross-validation.
   - **Step 2:** Augment the latent space using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a continuous probability distribution of valid spectral coefficients. This step smooths the manifold to handle sparse photometric inputs.
   - **Step 3:** Generate synthetic photometric data by convolving each augmented spectrum with the effective throughput curves of the target instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters). Add Gaussian noise (e.g., 20%) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.
   - **Step 4:** Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map the synthetic photometry to the latent PCA coefficients. This model serves as the "encoder."
   - **Step 5:** Train a secondary classifier to assign taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic) based on the latent coefficients, improving classification accuracy.

2. **Inference Phase:**
   - **Input:** Sparse photometric measurements of a TNO.
   - **Step 1:** Pass the photometric data through the trained encoder to predict the latent PCA coefficients.
   - **Step 2:** Project the predicted coefficients back into the spectral space using the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - **Step 3:** Compute credible intervals and perform taxonomic classification based on the reconstructed spectrum.
   - **Output:** Reconstructed spectrum, credible intervals, and taxonomic probabilities.

3. **Applications:**
   - **Survey Optimization:** Evaluate different filter configurations to identify the most informative set for TNO spectroscopy.
   - **Outlier Detection:** Analyze posterior distributions of latent variables to identify unusual spectral types.
   - **Spectral Mapping:** Use the reconstructed spectra to study the compositional distribution of TNO populations across the Solar System.

This algorithm effectively bridges sparse photometry and detailed spectral analysis, enabling robust reconstruction and classification of TNO spectra while accounting for uncertainties and propagation of measurement errors.

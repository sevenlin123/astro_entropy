**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework**

**Objective**: Reconstruct high-resolution spectra of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric measurements using a Bayesian framework.

**Steps**:

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a seed set of 51 TNO spectra categorized into dominant taxonomic classes (Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude rare or singular spectral types to ensure a representative and balanced training set.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the training spectra to extract principal components (PCs) representing major spectral variations.
   - The number of PCs (K) is determined empirically, typically 4-5 for dimensionality reduction and up to 10 for improved fidelity.

3. **Latent Space Definition**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to smooth the distribution of PCA coefficients, creating a continuous latent manifold.
   - The bandwidth for KDE is optimized (e.g., h ≈ 0.5) to balance local fidelity and global smoothness without overfitting.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - Simulate photometric measurements by convolving the augmented spectra with JWST/NIRCam filter responses.
   - Inject noise at a 20% uncertainty level to account for observational realism.

5. **AutoML Regression Model Training**:
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) to map sparse photometric inputs to latent PCA coefficients.
   - Utilize automated machine learning (AutoGluon) for model selection, ensuring robust performance without manual tuning.

6. **Bayesian Inference for Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - For new photometric observations, predict the latent coefficients using the trained AutoML model.
   - Apply inverse PCA transformation to reconstruct the full spectrum, constrained by the learned manifold.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Project the predicted latent coefficients into a classifier trained on the taxonomic labels of the training set.
   - Output probability distributions over spectral types, enabling confident classification.

8. **Performance Evaluation**:
   - Conduct Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess RMSE and credible interval coverage for spectral reconstruction.
   - Measure taxonomic classification accuracy by comparing predicted labels with true categories.

9. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**:
   - Identify optimal JWST/NIRCam filter configurations by evaluating discriminative power across taxonomic groups.
   - Detect rare spectral types or outliers by analyzing deviations from the main latent manifold clusters.

**Output**:
- Reconstructed high-resolution spectra with uncertainty estimates.
- Probabilistic taxonomic classifications.
- Optimized filter configurations for future surveys.
- Identification of anomalous spectral types.

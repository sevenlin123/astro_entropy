**Algorithm Description: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a seed set of reference TNO spectra (e.g., 51 spectra) representative of known taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude rare or outlier spectra to focus on dominant taxonomic groups.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Perform PCA on the seed spectra to reduce dimensionality. The number of principal components (K) is determined empirically, typically 4-5 for basic structure and 8-10 for improved fidelity.
   - The PCA produces eigenvectors (principal components) that represent major spectral variations.

3. **Latent Space Construction**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA latent coefficients to create a continuous probability distribution. This distribution represents a smoothed version of the spectral manifold.
   - This step augments the training data, allowing the model to generalize beyond the limited seed set.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - Forward model the spectra by convolving them with the instrument's filter throughput curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to simulate photometric measurements.
   - Introduce Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% uncertainty) to mimic real photometric observations, enhancing the model's robustness.

5. **AutoMLRegressor Training**:
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) using AutoML (e.g., AutoGluon) to map sparse photometric inputs to PCA latent coefficients.
   - The training data consists of noisy synthetic photometry paired with corresponding PCA targets.

6. **Inverse PCA Transform**:
   - Use the trained AutoML model to predict latent coefficients from new photometric observations.
   - Apply the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum from these coefficients.

7. **Taxonomy Classification**:
   - Project the latent coefficients into a classifier trained on the taxonomic labels of the seed spectra.
   - Classify new observations based on their latent positions, providing probabilities for each taxonomic type.

8. **Validation and Outlier Detection**:
   - Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and credible interval coverage.
   - Identify outliers by comparing posterior distributions of latent coefficients against the manifold of typical TNOs.

9. **Applications**:
   - **Survey Optimization**: Evaluate different filter configurations to maximize information yield for taxonomic区分.
   - **Outlier Detection**: Target objects with spectral reconstructions inconsistent with the manifold for detailed follow-up.

**Key Features**:
- Utilizes a Bayesian framework combining PCA and AutoML for robust spectral reconstruction.
- Handles sparse and noisy photometry effectively by leveraging latent space regularization.
- Provides probabilistic taxonomic classification alongside spectral reconstruction.
- Designed for scalability and applicability to large-scale surveys.

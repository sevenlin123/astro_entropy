### Algorithm Description: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework

**Step-by-Step Algorithm:**

1. **Training Data Preparation:**
   - Collect a sample of reference TNO spectra (e.g., 51 high-quality near-IR spectra).
   - Exclude rare spectral types and outliers to focus on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Apply PCA to the training spectra to create a low-dimensional latent space.
   - Determine the number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for classification, 8-10 for detailed reconstruction) capturing significant spectral variability.

3. **Density Estimation (Kernel Density Estimation - KDE):**
   - Project the PCA coefficients of the training spectra into a latent space.
   - Use KDE to smooth the latent space, creating a continuous manifold that approximates the underlying distribution of spectral types.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation:**
   - Simulate photometric measurements for each augmented spectrum by convolving with the instrument's filter response curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).
   - Introduce noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to account for observational uncertainties, ensuring the model generalizes well to real data.

5. **Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) Training:**
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometric inputs to the latent PCA coefficients.
   - Use the synthetic photometry and corresponding latent coefficients as training data.

6. **Inverse PCA Transformation:**
   - After predicting latent coefficients from new photometric data, apply the inverse PCA transformation to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - This step ensures the reconstructed spectrum inherits the physical characteristics of the training data.

7. **Taxonomic Classification:**
   - Train a secondary classifier on the latent space to predict the probability distribution of spectral types (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).
   - Classify new objects based on their latent coefficients, providing probabilistic taxonomic assignments.

8. **Filter Optimization:**
   - Identify optimal filter configurations (e.g., F090W, F115W, F410M, F460M) for maximizing information content in photometric surveys.
   - Evaluate each filter's contribution to distinguishing spectral types, ensuring efficient and effective data collection.

9. **Outlier Detection:**
   - Utilize the continuous latent manifold to detect unusual spectral types by constraining intermediate models to physically plausible states.
   - Highlight peculiar objects (e.g., 2006 RJ103, 2011 SO277) that deviate significantly from the expected spectral features.

10. **Reconstruction and Validation:**
    - For each object, use its photometric data to predict latent coefficients, decode to a spectrum, and validate against known spectra (if available).
    - Measure reconstruction accuracy using metrics like RMSE and correlation coefficients.

11. **Survey Planning and Follow-Up:**
    - Optimize survey strategies using the identified optimal filters and focus on objects flagged as outliers for detailed spectroscopic follow-up.

### Output:

The algorithm provides reconstructed full spectra and taxonomic classifications for TNOs based on sparse photometric data, enabling efficient exploration of the outer Solar System.

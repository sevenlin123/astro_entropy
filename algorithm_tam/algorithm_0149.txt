### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Training Phase**:
   - **Input**: Set of reference TNO spectra.
   - **Step 1**: Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the reference spectra to extract principal components (latent space).
   - **Step 2**: Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to smooth the latent space, creating a continuous probability distribution.
   - **Step 3**: Generate synthetic photometry by convolving the spectra with the instrument's filter response curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - **Step 4**: Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) into synthetic photometry to simulate realistic observations.
   - **Step 5**: Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometry to latent space coefficients.
   - **Step 6**: Train a secondary classifier in the latent space for taxonomic classification.

2. **Inference Phase**:
   - **Input**: Sparse photometric measurements of a new object.
   - **Step 1**: Use the trained AutoML regressor to predict latent coefficients.
   - **Step 2**: Project the latent coefficients through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - **Step 3**: Optionally, classify the object into taxonomic categories using the latent space classifier.

3. **Validation and Applications**:
   - Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction fidelity and taxonomic accuracy.
   - Optimize photometric survey configurations by evaluating the information content of filter sets.
   - Detect outliers by analyzing posterior probabilities in the latent space.

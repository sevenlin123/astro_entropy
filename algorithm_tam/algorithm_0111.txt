### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework for TNOs from Sparse Photometry

**Objective:**  
Reconstruct high-resolution near-infrared (NIR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric measurements using a Bayesian framework.

**Steps:**

1. **Training Data Preparation:**
   - Collect a seed set of 51 high-quality NIR spectra of TNOs belonging to dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude rare spectral types and outliers to maintain a "clean" latent space.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Apply PCA to the seed spectra to extract principal components (PCs) representing dominant spectral variations.
   - Determine the optimal number of PCs (4-5 for taxonomic classification; 8-10 for high-fidelity reconstruction).

3. **Latent Space Definition:**
   - Project the seed spectra onto the extracted PCs to form latent coefficients.
   - Augment the latent space using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth probability distribution, enabling interpolation between discrete spectra.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation:**
   - Convolve the augmented spectra with the filter curves of the target instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to compute synthetic photometric magnitudes.
   - Inject Gaussian noise (20% relative uncertainty) into the synthetic photometry to simulate realistic observational uncertainties.

5. **AutoML Regression Model Training:**
   - Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees using the synthetic photometry as inputs and latent coefficients as targets.
   - The model learns the non-linear mapping from sparse photometry to latent space.

6. **Taxonomic Classifier Training:**
   - Train a secondary AutoML classifier on the latent coefficients to predict taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics, Methanol).

7. **Forward Inference Pipeline:**
   - For new photometric observations:
     - Use the trained AutoML regressor to predict latent coefficients.
     - Project the latent coefficients through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full NIR spectrum.
     - Simultaneously, use the classifier to determine the probability distribution of taxonomic labels.

8. **Validation and Evaluation:**
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy, empirical credible-interval coverage, and taxonomic classification confidence.
   - Measure the information content of different filter combinations to optimize future photometric surveys.

9. **Outlier Detection:**
   - Identify objects with posterior probabilities significantly deviating from the main spectral manifold as potential outliers or rare spectral types.

10. **Survey Optimization:**
    - Evaluate the information content of NIRCam filter configurations to prioritize optimal filter sets for high-fidelity spectral reconstruction and taxonomic classification.

**Output:**
- Reconstructed NIR spectra from sparse photometry.
- Probabilistic taxonomic classification of TNOs.
- Detection of rare spectral types and outliers.
- Optimized filter configurations for photometric surveys.

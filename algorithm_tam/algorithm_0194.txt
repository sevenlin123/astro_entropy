### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework

1. **Training Phase**:
   - **Step 1**: Collect and preprocess a set of reference TNO spectra.
   - **Step 2**: Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to the reference spectra to define a low-dimensional latent space. The eigenvectors represent spectral variability.
   - **Step 3**: Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to create a continuous probability distribution, augmenting the training data.
   - **Step 4**: Forward model the spectra through the instrument response functions (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) to generate synthetic photometric data with added noise (20% uncertainty).
   - **Step 5**: Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometry to PCA coefficients.
   - **Step 6**: Train a secondary AutoML classifier to partition the latent space into taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).

2. **Inference Phase**:
   - **Step 1**: For new photometric observations, use the trained AutoML regressor to predict PCA coefficients.
   - **Step 2**: Project the predicted coefficients through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - **Step 3**: Simultaneously classify the object by evaluating its position in the latent space against predefined taxonomic boundaries.
   - **Step 4**: Propagate uncertainties from the photometric measurements through the latent space to the reconstructed spectrum using the variance-covariance matrix.

3. **Validation**:
   - **Step 1**: Perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction fidelity (RMSE, Pearson's r) and uncertainty propagation (credible interval coverage, uncertainty RMSE).
   - **Step 2**: Evaluate taxonomic classification accuracy (precision, recall, F1 score) across the dataset.

### Summary
The framework combines dimensionality reduction, probabilistic modeling, and machine learning to reconstruct high-resolution TNO spectra from sparse photometry, enabling taxonomic classification and outlier detection.

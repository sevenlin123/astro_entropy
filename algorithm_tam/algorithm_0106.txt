### Core Computational Algorithm for Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Latent-Space Spectral Reconstruction**

**Objective**: Reconstruct high-resolution spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric measurements using a Bayesian framework and Principal Component Analysis (PCA).

**Steps**:

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a representative sample of TNO near-infrared spectra, focusing on dominant taxonomic types (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude outlier spectra (e.g., Centaurs, Dwarf Planets) to maintain a consistent latent space.
   - Preprocess the spectral data by removing outliers, normalizing, and mean-centering.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Decompose the preprocessed spectral dataset into principal components to define a low-dimensional latent space.
   - The number of components (K) is determined based on explained variance and cross-validation results (e.g., K = 4-10).

3. **Latent Space Augmentation**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to generate synthetic latent points, expanding the training dataset.
   - The KDE bandwidth is optimized (e.g., h ≈ 0.5) to balance local fidelity and global smoothness.

4. **AutoML Regressor Training**:
   - Train an ensemble model (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to regress from sparse photometric measurements to latent space coefficients.
   - Synthetic photometry is generated by convolving augmented spectra with instrument response functions (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) and adding noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty).

5. **Inverse PCA Transformation**:
   - Apply the inverse PCA transform to the predicted latent coefficients to reconstruct the full spectra.
   - The reconstruction inherits the physical smoothness and absorption features of the training population.

6. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Train a secondary classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic categories (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic, Methanol).
   - Classify new objects using the latent coefficients derived from photometric data.

7. **Cross-Validation**:
   - Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy.
   - Evaluate metrics such as RMSE, credible interval coverage, and classification accuracy.

8. **Filter Information Content Analysis**:
   - Compute mutual information between photometric bands and latent space to determine the most informative filters.
   - Optimize filter configurations for survey design, enhancing spectral reconstruction fidelity.

9. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Identify objects with latent coefficients outside the expected distribution.
   - Flag rare or unusual spectral types for targeted spectroscopic follow-up.

10. **Survey Optimization**:
    - Use the framework to design efficient photometric surveys by selecting optimal filter sets.
    - Maximize the scientific yield by prioritizing observations that provide the highest information content.

**Output**: Reconstructed high-resolution spectra, taxonomic classifications, and identified outliers from sparse photometric inputs.

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction of TNOs from Sparse Photometry**

**Steps:**

1. **Training Data Preparation:**
   - Collect a sample of near-infrared (NIR) spectra of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) representative of dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to focus on the majority population for PCA analysis.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Apply PCA to the collected spectra to identify dominant eigenvectors capturing spectral variability.
   - Use the top K principal components (K = 4-5 for classification, up to K=8-10 for improved fidelity) to define a low-dimensional latent space.

3. **Data Augmentation with KDE:**
   - Project the seed spectra onto the PCA latent space.
   - Perform Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to smooth the latent distribution, generating synthetic latent vectors that fill intermediate regions of the manifold.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation:**
   - Convolve the augmented spectra with filter curves of the target instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to compute synthetic photometric measurements.
   - Introduce Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.

5. **Model Training:**
   - Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map noisy photometric inputs to the latent coefficients derived from PCA.
   - Simultaneously train a classifier within the latent space to predict taxonomic categories (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics, Methanol).

6. **Reconstruction Process:**
   - For new observations, project photometric data into the latent space using the trained regressor.
   - Apply inverse PCA transformation to the latent coefficients to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - Use the latent space classifier to determine the taxonomic classification of the object.

7. **Validation and Application:**
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and fidelity.
   - Apply the framework to detect rare spectral types and optimize survey configurations by evaluating information content of different filter sets.

**Output:**
- Reconstructed high-resolution spectra from sparse photometry.
- Probabilistic taxonomic classification of TNOs.
- Optimized filter configurations for future surveys.
- Detection of rare or peculiar spectral types.

This algorithm efficiently bridges sparse photometric data with detailed spectral information, enabling robust characterization of TNO surface compositions in large-scale surveys.

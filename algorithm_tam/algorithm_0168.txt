### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Objective:**  
Reconstruct high-resolution near-infrared spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric measurements using a Bayesian framework.

**Steps:**

1. **Training Data Preparation:**  
   - Collect a set of reference TNO spectra representative of known spectral types (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).  
   - Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to focus on the dominant population.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**  
   - Apply PCA to the reference spectra to derive a low-dimensional latent space (principal components).  
   - The first few principal components (4-5) capture the major spectral variability relevant for taxonomic classification, while 8-10 components improve reconstruction fidelity.

3. **Data Augmentation:**  
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to smooth the latent space distributions, creating synthetic spectra that fill gaps between observed data.  
   - Inject Gaussian noise (relative uncertainty ~20%) into synthetic photometry derived from these augmented spectra to simulate real-world observational noise.

4. **AutoML Regressor Training:**  
   - Train an ensemble of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) to map sparse photometric inputs to PCA latent coefficients.  
   - The regressor learns the non-linear relationship between photometry and latent space.

5. **Inverse PCA Transform:**  
   - Use the trained AutoML regressor to predict latent coefficients from new photometric observations.  
   - Apply the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full high-resolution spectrum.

6. **Taxonomic Classification (Optional):**  
   - Train a secondary classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic labels (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type) based on the predicted latent coefficients.

7. **Validation (LOOCV):**  
   - Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation to assess reconstruction accuracy and empirical credible interval coverage (~95%).  
   - Measure Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and classification accuracy.

8. **Applications:**  
   - **Survey Optimization:** Analyze information content of photometric filters to identify optimal configurations for future observations.  
   - **Outlier Detection:** Identify objects with unusual spectral features by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models.

**Output:**  
- Reconstructed high-resolution spectra with uncertainty estimates.  
- Probabilistic taxonomic classification of TNOs.  
- Optimized filter configurations for near-infrared surveys.  
- Detection of rare spectral types and outliers.

**Algorithm Description: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework**

1. **Input Data Preparation**:
   - Obtain sparse photometric measurements of TNOs.
   - Use these measurements as inputs for the reconstruction process.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Training**:
   - Train a PCA model on a reference set of near-infrared spectra of TNOs.
   - This step defines a low-dimensional latent space that captures the major spectral variations of the training set.

3. **Density Estimation of Latent Space**:
   - Apply Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA latent coefficients derived from the training spectra.
   - This creates a smooth probability distribution over the latent space, enabling generation of synthetic spectra.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - For each synthetic spectrum, compute the expected photometric values by convolving the spectrum with the instrumental filter responses (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).
   - Inject noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) into the synthetic photometry to simulate realistic observational conditions.

5. **Training the AutoML Regressor**:
   - Use the synthetic photometry and corresponding PCA latent coefficients as training data.
   - Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoGluon framework) to map sparse photometry to latent space coordinates.

6. **Reconstruction Process**:
   - For new photometric observations, pass the measurements through the trained AutoML regressor to predict latent coefficients.
   - Project these coefficients back into the high-dimensional spectral space using inverse PCA transformation to obtain the reconstructed spectrum.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Utilize a secondary classifier trained on the latent space to predict the taxonomic type (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics) of the object.
   - This classification is based on the latent coefficients derived from the photometric data.

8. **Uncertainty Propagation and Outlier Detection**:
   - Compute uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra by analyzing the variability in the latent space predictions.
   - Identify outliers by comparing the reconstructed spectra against the expected manifold defined by the training data.

9. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Evaluate different photometric filter configurations to determine their information content and effectiveness in spectral reconstruction.
   - Select optimal filter combinations that maximize reconstruction fidelity for specific spectral features or taxonomic types.

**Output**: Reconstructed high-resolution spectra, taxonomic classifications, and outlier detection for trans-Neptunian objects based on sparse photometric data.

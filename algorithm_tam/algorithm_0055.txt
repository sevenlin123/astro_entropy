**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

**Step-by-Step Description:**

1. **Training Data Preparation**:
   - Collect a seed set of TNO near-infrared spectra (e.g., 51 spectra).
   - Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to focus on dominant taxonomic classes.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the seed spectra to derive principal components (latent space).
   - The number of components (K) is determined empirically (e.g., 8–10 for high fidelity).

3. **Density Estimation in Latent Space**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth probability distribution in the latent space.
   - Adjust KDE bandwidth to balance smoothing and fidelity to the seed data.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - Convolve each augmented spectrum with the instrument's filter response (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric measurements.

5. **AutoML Model Training**:
   - Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoGluon) to regress from photometric inputs to latent coefficients.
   - Input features are photometric magnitudes in selected filters.

6. **LOOCV Validation**:
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation to assess reconstruction accuracy and uncertainty propagation.
   - Measure RMS deviation (spectral residual) between reconstructed and original spectra.

7. **Reconstruction and Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - For new photometric observations, predict latent coefficients using the trained AutoML model.
   - Apply inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - Use Monte Carlo sampling to estimate pixel-wise uncertainties.

8. **Taxonomic Classification**:
   - Train a secondary classifier in the latent space to predict taxonomic categories (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic).
   - Classify objects based on their latent coefficients.

9. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**:
   - Analyze information content of different filter combinations to optimize survey designs.
   - Detect outliers by identifying low-probability latent states in the manifold.

10. **Application**:
    - Use the framework for taxonomic classification, survey planning, and identifying rare spectral types from sparse photometry.

**Output**:
- Reconstructed spectra with uncertainties.
- Probabilistic taxonomic classifications.
- Optimized filter configurations for surveys.
- Outlier detection for spectroscopic follow-up.

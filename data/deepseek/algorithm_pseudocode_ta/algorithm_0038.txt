The algorithm can be broken down into several steps:

1. **Principal Component Decomposition**:
   - Start with a set of reference near-infrared spectra for trans-Neptunian objects.
   - Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce the dimensionality of the spectral data.
   - The goal is to capture the main modes of spectral variability, which will be used to reconstruct SEDs from sparse photometric data.

2. **Bayesian Inference Setup**:
   - Define a probabilistic model where the latent space is represented by the principal components obtained from PCA.
   - Establish a likelihood function that relates the observed photometric measurements to the latent variables (principal components).
   - Incorporate prior distributions over the latent variables based on domain knowledge.

3. **Forward Modeling**:
   - Create a forward model that converts the latent space representation (principal components) into the full spectral energy distribution (SED).
   - This model should account for the physical processes that produce the spectra, including any known correlations or dependencies between spectral features.

4. **Reconstruction Process**:
   - For each object of interest, use the observed sparse photometric measurements as inputs.
   - Apply Bayesian inference in the reduced latent space to sample the posterior distribution of the underlying spectral parameters.
   - The posterior samples are used to reconstruct the full SED, incorporating the uncertainties from the photometric measurements.

5. **Dimensionality Determination**:
   - Use leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to determine the optimal number of principal components required for accurate spectral reconstruction.
   - LOOCV helps validate the model's performance across different dimensionalities, ensuring that the selected number of components effectively captures the necessary spectral variability.

6. **Error Propagation and Uncertainty Quantification**:
   - Alongside the reconstruction of the SEDs, propagate the uncertainties from the photometric measurements through the model.
   - Calculate confidence intervals or credible intervals for the reconstructed spectra to assess the reliability of the estimates.

7. **Application to Specific Objects**:
   - For objects like the peculiar Neptune Trojans, apply the same reconstruction process but focus on low-probability intermediate models.
   - Adjust the selection criteria if necessary to better capture rare or unusual spectral types.

8. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Use the framework to evaluate the information content of different JWST/NIRCam filters.
   - Identify filter configurations that maximize the retention of spectral information, aiding in taxonomic classification and efficient survey planning.

This structured approach ensures that the method is both computationally efficient and statistically rigorous, bridging the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral reconstruction.

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Preprocess the dataset:**
   - Normalize each photometric band by dividing by the median of that band to remove offsets.
   - Scale all measurements to have unit variance to ensure consistent data magnitude.

2. **Train a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) model:**
   - Input the sample of near-infrared spectra into the PCA model.
   - Determine the optimal number of principal components ( PCs ) via cross-validation (e.g., 4-5 PCs for taxonomic classification).
   - Retain these PCs to capture dominant spectral variability.

3. **Construct the latent-space model:**
   - Project normalized photometric data into the PCA latent space.
   - Establish a Bayesian inference framework where photometric data inform the posterior distribution of latent variables representing underlying spectra.

4. **Estimate posterior distribution using MCMC sampling:**
   - Apply a suitable MCMC method (e.g., Hamiltonian Monte Carlo or No U-Turn Sampler).
   - Sample the latent variables to explore the posterior distribution, ensuring convergence and proper mixing.

5. **Reconstruct full spectra:**
   - Map MCMC-sampled latent variables back through the trained PCA model.
   - Combine the latent components linearly to obtain reconstructed spectra.

6. **Propagate uncertainties:**
   - Compute the covariance matrix of the latent variables from MCMC samples.
   - Use the covariance to determine uncertainty in reconstructed spectra.

**End of Algorithm**

1. Training phase
    1a. Collect a sufficiently large sample of TNO spectra from literature.
    1b. Perform PCA on the collected spectra to extract Principal Components.
    1c. Determine the optimal number of Principal Components based on explained variance and cross-validation metrics.
    1d. Build a probabilistic model of the spectral manifold in the reduced PC space, capturing the distribution of spectra.

2. Reconstruction phase
    2a. For each TNO object, input the sparse photometric measurements, which may include missing data or high uncertainty.
    2b. Convert the photometric measurements into flux densities, accounting for measurement errors and handling missing data appropriately.
    2c. Use the pre-trained PC model to perform Bayesian inference, estimating the most probable position of the object's spectrum in the PC space.
    2d. Reconstruct the full spectrum from the estimated Principal Component coefficients, incorporating uncertainties derived from the Bayesian inference step.
    2e. Sample the reconstructed spectrum at regular intervals, matching the observed range of the input photometry.

3. Uncertainty quantification
    3a. Propagate uncertainties from the photometric measurements through the Bayesian inference process.
    3b. Compute the total uncertainty on the reconstructed spectrum by combining uncertainties on the Principal Component coefficients and considering their covariance structure.
    3c. Calculate credible intervals for the reconstructed spectrum to ensure proper coverage based on the posterior distribution.

4. Additional features
    4a. Apply the framework to optimize NIR survey configurations by evaluating the information content of different filter combinations.
    4b. Implement outlier detection by monitoring Bayesian evidence or other statistical measures during the inference process.

The algorithm is structured as follows:

1. **Input Data**: Gather near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
2. **Preprocessing**:
   - Remove outliers from the spectral data.
   - Normalize the spectral measurements to ensure consistent scale.
3. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Decompose the preprocessed spectral dataset into principal components.
   - Determine the optimal number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for improved reconstruction fidelity).
4. **Probabilistic Latent-Space Model Construction**:
   - Develop a probabilistic model based on the selected principal components.
   - This model captures the latent structure of the spectral manifold.
5. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - For each TNO, use its sparse photometric measurements.
   - Map these measurements into the precomputed PCA-based latent space.
   - Conduct Bayesian inference within this latent space to estimate the underlying spectral components.
   - This step involves propagating measurement uncertainties through the model.
6. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Transform the estimated latent-space parameters back into the full spectral space.
7. **Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Calculate the uncertainties associated with the reconstructed spectra.
   - Ensure the credible intervals (e.g., 95%) adequately cover the spectral variations.
8. **Output**:
   - Reconstructed full spectra for each TNO.
   - Associated uncertainties quantified through Bayesian inference.

The algorithm iterates over each TNO, processing them individually to reconstruct their full spectra from sparse photometric data using the outlined steps.

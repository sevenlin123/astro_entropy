**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Preprocessing:**
   - Input: Observed photometric magnitudes (sparse data points)
   - Normalize the photometric data to account for instrumental biases and observational noise.

2. **PCA Decomposition:**
   - Training Data: Collection of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs)
   - Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the training spectra.
   - Determine the optimal number of Principal Components (PCs) using leave-one-out cross-validation.
     - Example: Retain 4-5 PCs for taxonomic classification; 8-10 PCs for high-fidelity reconstruction.

3. **Bayesian Inference:**
   - Represent the problem in the latent PC space.
   - Formulate the likelihood function linking photometric observations to the latent variables (PCs).
   - Define the prior distribution over the latent variables, incorporating known constraints on spectral variability.
   - Perform Bayesian inference to compute the posterior distribution of the latent variables given the photometric data.
     - This step involves updating the latent variables based on the observed photometry, considering measurement uncertainties and propagating them into the reconstruction.

4. **Spectrum Reconstruction:**
   - Using the inferred latent variables (posterior means of the PCs), apply the inverse PCA transformation.
   - Reconstruct the original spectrum by combining the principal components.

5. **Output:**
   - Reconstructed spectrum: Full spectral profile derived from the photometric data.
   - Posterior uncertainties: Quantified uncertainties in the reconstructed spectrum.

---

**Note:** This algorithm focuses solely on the core reconstruction framework, excluding additional steps like validation procedures, visualization, and outlier detection as per the user's request.

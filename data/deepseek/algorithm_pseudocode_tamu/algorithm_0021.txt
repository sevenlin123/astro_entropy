### Pseudocode Algorithm for Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction

```python
Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
    - Training Spectra: A collection of reference TNO spectra.
    - Observed Photometry: Sparse photometric measurements of a target TNO.
    - Filter Response: Instrument-specific filter responses (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
    - Noise Level: Relative reflectance uncertainty (e.g., 20%).

Output:
    - Reconstructed Spectrum: High-resolution spectral reconstruction.
    - Uncertainty Estimates: Probabilistic confidence intervals.
    - Taxonomic Classification: Predicted spectral type probabilities.

Steps:

1. **Preprocessing and PCA Initialization**
    1.1. Train PCA on the reference spectra to extract principal components.
    1.2. Determine the number of principal components (k) based on explained variance and LOOCV results (typically 8–10 for high fidelity).

2. **Latent Space Construction**
    2.1. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients of the reference spectra to create a smooth continuous manifold.
    2.2. Adjust KDE bandwidth to balance between overfitting and oversmoothing.

3. **Forward Modeling and Synthetic Data Generation**
    3.1. For each synthetic latent vector:
        a. Generate a full spectrum using inverse PCA.
        b. Convolve the spectrum with filter responses to simulate photometric magnitudes.
        c. Add Gaussian noise to simulate observational uncertainty.
    3.2. Combine synthetic and real data for training.

4. **Training AutoML Regressor**
    4.1. Train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (e.g., XGBoost) to map photometric inputs to latent coefficients.
    4.2. Use synthetic data to augment training and improve generalization.

5. **Reconstruction Pipeline**
    5.1. For new photometric observations:
        a. Predict latent coefficients using the trained regressor.
        b. Sample from the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients using Gaussian Copula to account for correlations.
    5.2. Project the latent coefficients through inverse PCA to reconstruct the full spectrum.
    5.3. Calculate credible intervals from the posterior samples.

6. **Taxonomic Classification**
    6.1. Use a secondary classifier trained on the latent space to predict taxonomic probabilities.
    6.2. Classify the target into predefined spectral types (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organics).

7. **Validation and Uncertainty Assessment**
    7.1. Compute RMSE, PCC, and empirical coverage of credible intervals.
    7.2. Ensure posterior uncertainties are well-calibrated.

8. **Applications**
    8.1. Optimize filter configurations for future surveys.
    8.2. Detect outliers by identifying low-probability regions in the latent space.
```

### Explanation of Key Steps

1. **PCA Initialization**: Extracts the main sources of spectral variation from reference data, reducing dimensionality.
2. **KDE for Latent Space**: Creates a smooth probability distribution of plausible latent states, aiding stable regression training.
3. **Forward Modeling**: Simulates photometric measurements from synthetic spectra, incorporating noise for realistic training.
4. **AutoML Training**: Efficiently maps sparse photometry to latent space using ensemble models, avoiding overfitting.
5. **Reconstruction and Uncertainty**: Generates full spectra with probabilistic uncertainties, ensuring reliable confidence intervals.
6. **Classification**: Enhances interpretability by assigning taxonomic labels based on latent features.
7. **Validation**: Ensures accuracy and reliability through rigorous metrics.
8. **Practical Applications**: Facilitates survey planning and identifies anomalous objects.

Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
    - Photometric measurements (m) for a trans-Neptunian object
    - Pre-trained Principal Component Analysis (PCA) model with eigencomponents
    - Synthetic photometry generated from forward modeling with added noise

Output:
    - Reconstructed high-resolution spectrum (S)
    - Probabilistic taxonomic classification
    - Uncertainty quantification for spectral features

Steps:

1. **Latent Space Definition**:
    a. Apply PCA to reference TNO spectra to obtain principal components.
    b. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to model the probability distribution of latent coefficients.
    c. Augment training data with synthetic samples from the continuous manifold.

2. **Forward Modeling**:
    a. Simulate photometric measurements for each augmented spectrum using target instrument filtersthroughput curves.
    b. Inject Gaussian noise (relative reflectance uncertainty ~20%) to mimic observational uncertainties.
    c. Construct implicit likelihood mapping between photometry and latent space.

3. **Training Phase**:
    a. Train an AutoML regressor (ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees) to map sparse photometry to latent coefficients.
    b. Validate using Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to determine optimal number of principal components (k=8).

4. **Inference**:
    a. For new photometric data, predict latent coefficients using the trained regressor.
    b. Project latent coefficients through inverse PCA transform to reconstruct full spectrum.
    c. Sample joint posterior distribution of latent variables using Gaussian Copula to quantify uncertainties.

5. **Taxonomic Classification**:
    a. Classify predicted latent coefficients using a pre-trained classifier within the manifold.
    b. Assign probability distribution over spectral types (e.g., H2O, CO2, Organic, Methanol).

6. **Survey Optimization**:
    a. Evaluate sensitivity of latent components to different filters.
    b. Identify optimal filter combinations (e.g., F090W, F115W, F410M, F460M) for maximizing spectral information content.

7. **Outlier Detection**:
    a. Monitor posterior distribution of latent coefficients during reconstruction.
    b. Detect objects whose posterior falls outside high-density regions of the manifold.
    c. Constrain photometry to select low-probability intermediate models for rare spectral types.

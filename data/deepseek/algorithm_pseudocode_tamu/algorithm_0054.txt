Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Inputs:
    - Sparse photometric measurements (e.g., magnitudes or fluxes)
    - Training set of reference spectra (used to construct the latent space)
    - Filter response curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters)

Outputs:
    - Reconstructed full spectrum
    - Probabilistic constraints (credible intervals)
    - Taxonomic classification

Steps:

1. Preprocessing:
    a. Normalize photometric measurements to a common scale (e.g., reflectance).
    b. Extract features from photometric data (e.g., magnitudes in specific bands).

2. Construct Latent Space:
    a. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the training spectra to obtain principal components (PCs).
    b. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to model the prior distribution of spectra.

3. Forward Modeling:
    a. Simulate synthetic photometry for each augmented spectrum by convolving with instrument-specific filter responses.
    b. Add Gaussian noise to synthetic photometry to account for observational uncertainties (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty).

4. TrainRegressor:
    a. Use AutoML (e.g., XGBoost) to train a regressor mapping photometric inputs to latent PCA coefficients.
    b. Augment training data with synthetic samples generated from the latent manifold to improve model generalization.

5. Bayesian Inference:
    a. For each new photometric observation:
        i. Predict latent coefficients using the trained regressor.
        ii. Sample from the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients using Gaussian Copula.
        iii. Project sampled coefficients through inverse PCA to reconstruct full spectra.

6. Uncertainty Quantification:
    a. Compute credible intervals for reconstructed spectra based on posterior samples.

7. TaxonomyClassification:
    a. Apply trained classifier on latent coefficients to predict taxonomic probabilities.

8. Validation:
    a. Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and fidelity.
    b. Validate taxonomic predictions against known classifications.

9. Applications:
    a. Optimize filter configurations by evaluating information content in the latent space.
    b. Detect outliers by identifying high reconstruction errors or low classification probabilities.

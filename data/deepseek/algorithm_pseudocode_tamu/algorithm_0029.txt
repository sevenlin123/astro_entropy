### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Step 1: Training Data Preparation**
1. Collect a reference dataset of TNO near-infrared spectra (e.g., DiSCo-TNO sample).
2. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the spectral data to obtain principal components (PCs) and latent coefficients.

**Step 2: Latent Space Definition**
1. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA-derived latent coefficients to create a continuous probability distribution in the latent space.
2. Augment the training data by sampling from the KDE to generate synthetic latent vectors.

**Step 3: Synthetic Photometry Generation**
1. For each augmented spectrum, simulate photometric observations by convolving with the instrument's filter response curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).
2. Inject Gaussian noise with a specified relative uncertainty (e.g., 20%) into the synthetic photometry to account for observational errors.

**Step 4: Model Training**
1. Train an automated machine learning regressor (e.g., AutoGluon) to map sparse photometric observations to latent space coordinates.
2. Simultaneously train a classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic classes (e.g., Water, CO₂, Organic types).

**Step 5: Inference Phase**
1. For new photometric observations:
   - Use the trained regressor to predict latent space coordinates and their uncertainties.
   - Project the predicted latent coordinates through the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
2. Sample the joint posterior distribution of latent variables using a Gaussian Copula to generate an ensemble of reconstructed spectra and quantify uncertainties.

**Step 6: Taxonomic Classification**
1. Apply the trained classifier to the predicted latent coordinates to determine the probability distribution over taxonomic classes.

**Step 7: Performance Evaluation**
1. Assess reconstruction fidelity using Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) on key spectral features.
2. Validate uncertainty quantification by checking credible interval coverage.
3. Measure taxonomic classification accuracy and confidence.

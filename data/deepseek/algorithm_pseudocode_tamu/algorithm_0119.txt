Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. Input: Sparse photometric measurements of a trans-Neptunian object (TNO)

2. Precompute:
   a. Train a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) model on a reference set of TNO spectra.
   b. Determine the optimal number of principal components (K) through cross-validation.

3. Forward Modeling:
   a. Simulate photometric observations by convolving reference spectra with instrument-specific filter responses.
   b. Inject Gaussian noise (relative uncertainty ~20%) into simulated photometry to account for observational uncertainties.

4. Data Augmentation:
   a. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA latent space to generate synthetic training data.
   b. Ensure synthetic data maintains physical plausibility and fills intermediate regions of the spectral manifold.

5. Training Phase:
   a. Train an AutoML regressor (ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees) to map sparse photometry to latent PCA coefficients.
   b. Apply early stopping and model averaging to prevent overfitting.

6. Inference Phase:
   a. Use the trained regressor to predict latent coefficients from new photometric observations.
   b. Project the latent coefficients back to the full spectrum using the inverse PCA transform.

7. Uncertainty Quantification:
   a. Sample the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients using a Gaussian Copula framework.
   b. Generate an ensemble of reconstructed spectra to compute credible intervals.

8. Taxonomic Classification:
   a. Classify the latent coefficients into predefined spectral types (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic).
   b. Output probability distribution over taxonomic categories.

9. Output: 
   a. Reconstructed spectrum with uncertainties.
   b. Probabilistic taxonomic classification.
   c. Identification of outliers based on posterior probabilities.

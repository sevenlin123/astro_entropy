**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Training Phase:**
   - **Input:** Reference TNO spectra, sparse photometry data.
   - **Step 1:** Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the reference spectra to extract principal components.
   - **Step 2:** Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to model the probability distribution of the PCA coefficients.
   - **Step 3:** Generate synthetic training data by sampling from the KDE and forward modeling with added noise.
   - **Step 4:** Train an automated machine learning regressor (e.g., AutoGluon) to map sparse photometry to PCA coefficients.
   - **Step 5:** Validate using leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction accuracy and credible interval coverage.

2. **Inference Phase:**
   - **Input:** New sparse photometric observations.
   - **Step 1:** Use the trained regressor to predict the latent PCA coefficients.
   - **Step 2:** Apply inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   - **Step 3:** Optionally, classify the spectrum into taxonomic categories using a secondary classifier trained on the latent space.

3. **Uncertainty Quantification:**
   - **Step 1:** Sample the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients using a Gaussian Copula framework.
   - **Step 2:** Propagate uncertainties through the inverse PCA transform to generate confidence intervals for the reconstructed spectrum.

4. **Outlier Detection:**
   - **Step 1:** Compare predicted latent coefficients against the KDE-based density estimates.
   - **Step 2:** Flag objects with coefficients falling outside statistically significant thresholds for further investigation.

This algorithm enables the reconstruction of high-fidelity spectra from sparse photometry, quantifies uncertainties, and detects unusual spectral types.

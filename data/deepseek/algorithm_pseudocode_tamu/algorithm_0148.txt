Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. Preprocessing:
   a. Obtain a sample of reference near-infrared spectra of TNOs.
   b. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the sample to derive principal components (latent space).
   
2. Training:
   a. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) with a Gaussian kernel on the PCA coefficients to create a smooth probability distribution in the latent space.
   b. Generate synthetic photometry by convolving the reference spectra with the instrument response functions (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) and adding 20% Gaussian noise.
   c. Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees) to map sparse photometry to latent PCA coefficients.
   
3. Inference:
   a. For new photometric observations:
      i. Use the trained regressor to predict latent coefficients.
      ii. Apply inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
      iii. Sample the joint posterior of latent coefficients using Gaussian Copula to generate confidence intervals.
   b. Perform taxonomic classification by predicting class probabilities in the latent space.
   
4. Validation:
   a. Conduct Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to assess reconstruction fidelity and uncertainty calibration.
   
5. Applications:
   a. For survey optimization:
      i. Evaluate information content of different filter configurations.
   b. For outlier detection:
      i. Identify objects with posterior probabilities outside typical distributions.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Initialize Training Data**:
   - Collect a reference set of near-infrared spectra for trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs), specifically focusing on dominant taxonomic classes (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics).
   - Exclude rare or outlier spectra to ensure the PCA captures the primary spectral variability.

2. **Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the training spectra to extract principal components (PCs) that represent the major modes of spectral variability.
   - Determine the number of PCs (e.g., 4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for improved reconstruction fidelity).

3. **Define the Latent Space Prior**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to create a continuous probability distribution over the latent space.
   - This prior represents plausible spectral variations based on the training data.

4. **Generate Synthetic Training Data**:
   - For each spectrum in the latent space, forward-model the spectrum through the target instrument's filter set (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to produce synthetic photometry.
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) into the synthetic photometry to simulate observational uncertainties.

5. **Train AutoML Regressor**:
   - Use an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (e.g., XGBoost) to regress from noisy photometric bands to latent PCA coefficients.
   - The regressor learns the mapping from sparse photometry to the low-dimensional latent space.

6. **Calibrate the Posterior**:
   - Employ a Gaussian Copula framework to sample the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients.
   - Ensure the posterior respects the physical dependencies in the latent space, preventing unphysical spectral reconstructions.

7. **Reconstruct Spectra**:
   - For new photometric observations, predict the latent coefficients using the trained regressor.
   - Apply the inverse PCA transform to obtain the full reconstructed spectrum.

8. **Evaluate Reconstruction Quality**:
   - Compute metrics such as normalized Root Mean Square Deviation (nRMSE) and Maximum Absolute Error (MAE) to assess reconstruction fidelity.
   - Validate uncertainty estimates by checking empirical coverage of credible intervals.

9. **Detect Outliers and Classify Taxonomy**:
   - Analyze the probability distribution of latent coefficients to identify objects falling outside the typical manifold regions.
   - Classify spectral types using a secondary AutoML classifier trained on the latent space.

10. **Optimize Survey Configurations**:
    - Use the framework to evaluate the information content of different photometric filter combinations.
    - Select filter sets that maximize mutual information with spectral properties and improve taxonomic separation.

11. **Posterior Sampling and Uncertainty Propagation**:
    - Generate an ensemble of spectral reconstructions by sampling the posterior distribution.
    - Propagate uncertainties through the spectral features to provide reliable confidence intervals.

12. **Iterate and Validate**:
    - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to ensure generalization and calibrate model performance.
    - Adjust hyperparameters and latent space construction as needed based on validation results.

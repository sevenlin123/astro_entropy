### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Input:**  
- `obs_photometry`: Sparse photometric measurements of the object  
- `training_spectra`: Reference near-infrared spectra of TNOs  
- `filter_curves`: Effective throughput curves of the observation instrument (e.g., JWST NIRCam)  
- `n_components`: Number of principal components (e.g., 10)  

**Output:**  
- `reconstructed_spectrum`: High-resolution reconstructed spectrum  
- `credible_intervals`: Empirical credible intervals (e.g., 95%)  
- `taxonomy_prediction`: Probabilistic taxonomic classification  

**Steps:**

1. **Preprocessing and PCA Training**:
   - **Step 1**: Normalize and preprocess `training_spectra` to handle spectral variations.
   - **Step 2**: Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on `training_spectra` to obtain principal components (PCs) and latent coefficients.  
   
2. **Synthetic Data Augmentation**:
   - **Step 3**: Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the latent coefficients of the PCA to create a smooth, continuous manifold of synthetic spectra.
   - **Step 4**: Forward-model the synthetic spectra using the instrument's filter curves to generate synthetic photometric observations.

3. **Model Training**:
   - **Step 5**: Combine synthetic and real photometric data.
   - **Step 6**: Split the data into training and validation sets.
   - **Step 7**: Train an automated machine learning regressor (e.g., AutoGluon) to map photometric inputs to latent coefficients.

4. **Reconstruction Pipeline**:
   - **Step 8**: For new `obs_photometry`, predict latent coefficients using the trained regressor.
   - **Step 9**: Apply inverse PCA transform to the latent coefficients to reconstruct the full spectrum.

5. **Uncertainty Quantification**:
   - **Step 10**: Sample the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients using a Gaussian Copula to account for dependencies.
   - **Step 11**: Generate an ensemble of reconstructed spectra to compute credible intervals.

6. **Taxonomy Classification**:
   - **Step 12**: Use a trained classifier to predict the taxonomic class (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic) based on the latent coefficients.

7. **Validation and Outlier Detection**:
   - **Step 13**: Compare the reconstructed spectrum with the ground truth (if available) to assess fidelity and coverage of credible intervals.
   - **Step 14**: Flag objects with photometric observations inconsistent with the spectral manifold as outliers.

8. **Survey Optimization**:
   - **Step 15**: Evaluate different filter configurations to determine the optimal set for maximizing spectral reconstruction information content.

**End of Algorithm**

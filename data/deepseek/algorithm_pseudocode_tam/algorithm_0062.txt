Algorithm for Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. Initialize:
   - Input: Sparse photometric data of TNOs.
   - Output: Reconstructed spectral energy distribution and taxonomic classification.

2. Preprocess:
   a. Convert photometric data into normalized reflectance or flux units.
   b. Convolve data with telescope filter curves to simulate observed photometry.

3. Training Phase:
   a. Gather reference spectra of TNOs into a training set (e.g., 51 example spectra).
   b. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the training spectra to derive latent space (e.g., 4-10 principal components).
   c. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to create a smooth prior distribution.
   d. Augment the training data by generating synthetic latent vectors through sampling the KDE.

4. Model Training:
   a. Train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to map sparse photometry to latent coefficients.
   b. Train a secondary classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic labels.

5. Forward Inference:
   a. For new photometric observations:
      i. Pass photometric data through the trained regressor to obtain posterior distribution of latent coefficients.
      ii. Project latent coefficients back into full spectrum via inverse PCA transformation.
      iii. Generate reconstructed spectrum.

6. Validation:
   a. Compute Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) between reconstructed and actual spectra.
   b. Check credible interval coverage to assess uncertainty calibration.
   c. Evaluate taxonomic classification accuracy using confusion matrix and F1-score.

7. Applications:
   a. Optimize survey configurations by evaluating information content of NIRCam filters.
   b. Detect outliers by identifying low-probability regions in latent space.

8. Outputs:
   - High-resolution reconstructed spectrum.
   - Probabilistic taxonomic classification.
   - Quantitative measures of reconstruction fidelity and uncertainty.

End.

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Initialize Data Collection**
   - Gather a sample of reference TNO near-IR spectra.
   - Exclude outliers and rare spectral types to focus on dominant classes.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
   - Perform PCA on the reference spectra to derive principal components.
   - Select the number of components (e.g., 4-5 for core reconstruction, up to 10 for fidelity).
   - Obtain principal component coefficients for each spectrum.

3. **Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) Augmentation**
   - Project reference spectra onto the PCA latent space.
   - Use KDE to estimate the probability density of latent coefficients.
   - Set bandwidth to ensure smooth yet realistic spectral morphologies (e.g., h ≈ 0.5).

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**
   - For each augmented spectrum, convolve with JWST/NIRCam filter curves to compute reflectance in each band.
   - Introduce Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric measurements.

5. **Training the Encoder**
   - Use AutoML (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to regress from photometric data to latent coefficients.
   - The encoder maps sparse photometry to the latent space defined by PCA and KDE.

6. **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)**
   - For each spectrum in the reference set:
     - Remove the spectrum from the training data.
     - Test the encoder on the remaining data.
     - Measure reconstruction accuracy and uncertainty calibration.
     - Ensure no synthetic data influences validation.

7. **Spectral Reconstruction**
   - For new photometric observations:
     - Use the trained encoder to predict latent coefficients.
     - Apply inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
     - Propagate uncertainties from photometry to the reconstructed spectrum.

8. **Taxonomic Classification**
   - Train a classifier on the PCA latent space to predict spectral type probabilities.
   - Classify new objects based on their latent coefficients, identifying their taxonomic group confidently.

**End Algorithm**

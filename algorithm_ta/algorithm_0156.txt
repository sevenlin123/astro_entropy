**Algorithm for Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Input Training Data**: Acquire a dataset of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: 
   - Compute PCA on the training spectra to extract principal components (PCs).
   - PCs represent the main axes of spectral variability within the dataset.

3. **Determine Optimal Number of Components**:
   - Identify the number of PCs required to capture significant spectral variability (approximately 4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for accurate reconstruction).

4. **Spectral Manifold Modeling**:
   - Construct a spectral manifold using the selected PCs, capturing the essential spectral features of TNOs.

5. **Bayesian Inference in Reduced Space**:
   - Utilize Bayesian methods within the PCA-reduced space to reconstruct full spectra from sparse photometric measurements.
   - This step incorporates prior knowledge and quantifies uncertainties in the reconstruction process.

6. **Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Integrate measurement uncertainties from photometric data into the reconstruction, ensuring the propagated uncertainties accurately reflect the reliability of the reconstructed spectra.

7. **Applications**:
   - Perform taxonomic classification by analyzing the reconstructed spectra.
   - Detect outliers and peculiar objects, such as the Neptune Trojans, by identifying low-probability models within the spectral manifold.
   - Optimize future surveys by evaluating the information content of different filter configurations (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) to enhance spectral analysis.

This algorithm systematically bridges sparse photometric data with detailed spectral information, enabling robust characterization of TNOs' surface compositions and supporting large-scale survey initiatives.

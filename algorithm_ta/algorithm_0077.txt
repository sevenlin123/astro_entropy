The core computational algorithm for reconstructing the near-infrared spectra of trans-Neptunian objects from sparse photometric data involves the following steps:

1. **Preprocess the Data**: Collect and normalize the multiband photometric measurements of the trans-Neptunian objects to ensure uniformity and eliminate biases.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Apply PCA to a dataset of known near-infrared spectra to derive a set of principal components, capturing the main sources of spectral variability.

3. **Probabilistic Model Construction**: Develop a probabilistic model, such as a Gaussian Process, within the PCA-reduced latent space to represent the spectral manifold of the objects.

4. **Bayesian Inference**: Utilize Bayesian methods to estimate the spectral components of each object based on their photometric measurements, updating the model with observed data.

5. **Spectral Reconstruction**: Reconstruct the full near-infrared spectrum by combining the estimated components, leveraging the probabilistic model to incorporate uncertainties.

6. **Uncertainty Propagation**: Calculate how uncertainties in the photometric measurements influence the reconstructed spectrum, ensuring accurate uncertainty estimates.

7. **Output the Results**: Present the reconstructed spectrum along with confidence intervals derived from the propagated uncertainties.

This algorithm efficiently processes large datasets, enabling the statistical reconstruction of spectra and their uncertainties for numerous objects.

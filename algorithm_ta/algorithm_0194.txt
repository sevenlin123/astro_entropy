**Algorithm for Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction of Trans-Neptunian Objects**

**1. Preprocessing**

   **Step 1:** Collect a comprehensive training dataset composed of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

   **Step 2:** Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to the training dataset to derive a spectral manifold. This involves identifying principal components (PCs) that capture the majority of the spectral variability.

   **Step 3:** Archive the PCA model, including coefficients, eigenvectors, and explained variances, for subsequent use in processing individual objects.

**2. Processing Each Object**

   **a. Photometric Data Handling**

      **Step 1:** Retrieve the observed photometric fluxes for the object across all available bands.

      **Step 2:** Identify any missing photometric bands in the data.

      **Step 3:** Address missing bands through imputation methods, such as substituting with the mean or median of the available data or applying more sophisticated techniques like k-nearest neighbors (KNN) imputation.

   **b. Mapping to Spectral Manifold**

      **Step 1:** Utilize the precomputed PCA model to project the object's photometric data into the reduced spectral manifold. This step transforms the photometric measurements into the latent space of principal components.

   **c. Bayesian Inference**

      **Step 1:** Establish a Bayesian model where the latent variables (principal components) are inferred based on the observed photometric data. The model incorporates priors informed by the PCA analysis, such as variance contributions from each principal component.

      **Step 2:** Employ Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling or variational inference to compute the posterior distributions of the latent variables. This step quantifies the uncertainty in the inferred spectral parameters.

   **d. Spectrum Reconstruction**

      **Step 1:** Reconstruct the full spectrum by combining the posterior samples of the latent variables with the PCA basis vectors.

      **Step 2:** Determine the uncertainty (credible intervals) for each wavelength by propagating the uncertainty from the posterior distributions of the latent variables.

   **e. Output**

      **Step 1:** Store the reconstructed spectrum along with its associated uncertainties for further analysis or taxonomic classification.

This algorithm effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral reconstruction, enabling robust analysis of TNOs' compositional diversity.

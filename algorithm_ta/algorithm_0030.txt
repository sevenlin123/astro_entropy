1. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Apply PCA to a training set of near-infrared spectra to reduce the spectral data into a lower-dimensional principal component representation. This captures the dominant modes of spectral variability.

2. **Bayesian Inference in Reduced Space**: Use a probabilistic latent-space model, performing Bayesian inference within the reduced PCA space to reconstruct full spectra from sparse photometric measurements.

3. **Measurement Uncertainty Propagation**: Incorporate and propagate measurement uncertainties from the photometric data through the reconstruction process to quantify uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra.

4. **Spectral Manifold Modeling**: Model the spectral manifold of trans-Neptunian objects using the principal component representation, capturing the underlying structure and variability of the spectral data.

5. **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)**: Although simplified, validate the number of principal components used by applying LOOCV to determine the optimal dimensionality for spectral reconstruction.

6. **Survey Optimization**: Utilize the framework to assess the information content of different photometric filter combinations, identifying optimal configurations for future observations.

7. **Reconstruction of Rare Spectral Types**: Extend the method to handle cases where photometric data is insufficient or ambiguous by leveraging the continuous topological manifold of spectral models.

8. **Outlier Detection**: Detect and reconstruct rare or peculiar spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models from the manifold, allowing identification of unusual objects.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Input Data**: 
   - Near-infrared (near-IR) photometric measurements of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
   - A pre-trained principal component analysis (PCA) model derived from a sample of near-IR spectra.

2. **Training Phase**:
   - **PCA Training**: Use a training dataset of near-IR spectra to compute principal components (PCs). Retain approximately 4 to 5 PCs for taxonomic classification and 8 to 10 PCs for high-fidelity spectral reconstruction.
   - **Latent Space Representation**: Represent the spectral manifold of TNOs in a reduced latent space using the trained PCs.

3. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - Model the spectral manifold in the PCA latent space.
   - Perform Bayesian inference in this reduced space to reconstruct full spectra from photometric measurements.
   - Propagate measurement uncertainties through the Bayesian framework.

4. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - For each TNO, use its photometric measurements to infer the most probable spectral representation in the PCA space.
   - Reconstruct the full spectrum by mapping the inferred latent variables back to the original spectral space.

5. **Uncertainty Quantification**:
   - Calculate uncertainties for the reconstructed spectra by analyzing the posterior distribution of the latent variables.
   - Ensure that the empirical credible-interval coverage is approximately 95% across all wavelengths.

6. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Evaluate the information content of different JWST/NIRCam filters (e.g., F090W, F115W, F410M, F460M) using the trained framework.
   - Identify filter configurations that maximize the fidelity of spectral reconstruction and taxonomic classification.

7. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Apply the framework to detect and reconstruct rare spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models from the continuous topological manifold.

8. **Output**:
   - Reconstructed full spectra for each TNO with associated uncertainties.
   - Optimized filter configurations for future observations.
   - Identified outliers and peculiar spectral types.

This algorithm effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral reconstruction, enabling robust taxonomic classification and survey optimization for TNOs.

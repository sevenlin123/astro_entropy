The computational algorithm to implement the core reconstruction framework involves the following steps:

1. **Preprocessing**:
   - **Training Data Collection**: Aggregate a reference library of near-infrared (NIR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from existing studies.
   - **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Apply PCA to the collected spectra to derive the optimal basis for spectral representation, reducing the data into principal components.

2. **Probabilistic Modeling**:
   - **Generative Model Construction**: Utilize Gaussian Processes to build a generative model in the latent (reduced) space. This model captures the manifold structure of the spectral data.
   - **Mapping Definition**: Establish a mapping from the latent features to the observed photometric measurements using Bayesian Linear Regression. This step connects the reduced representation with the photometric data.
   - **Bayesian Inference**: Employ Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling to infer posterior distributions over the latent parameters, enabling probabilistic reconstruction of the spectra from sparse photometry.

3. **Postprocessing**:
   - **Uncertainty Quantification**: Assess the robustness of the reconstructed spectra through bootstrapping techniques.
   - **Credible Intervals Calculation**: Compute credible intervals to evaluate the uncertainty associated with each spectral reconstruction, ensuring the reliability of the results.

This structured approach effectively integrates dimensionality reduction and Bayesian inference to reconstruct high-fidelity spectra from sparse photometric data, bridging the gap between available photometric measurements and detailed spectroscopic analysis.

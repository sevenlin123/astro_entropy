The algorithm outlines a systematic approach to reconstructing spectra from sparse photometric data for trans-Neptunian objects using a probabilistic framework. Here's the structured algorithm:

1. **Prepare Training Dataset**: Gather a sample of near-infrared spectra of TNOs to serve as the training dataset.

2. **Compute Principal Components (PCA)**: Extract principal components from the training dataset to capture the major modes of spectral variability.

3. **Model Spectral Manifold**: Represent the spectral variability in a lower-dimensional latent space using the principal components.

4. **Bayesian Inference**: Perform Bayesian inference in the reduced space to reconstruct full spectra from sparse photometric measurements, incorporating measurement uncertainties.

5. **Determine Optimal Components**: Identify the optimal number of principal components (based on literature, e.g., 8–10) to balance fidelity and uncertainty calibration.

6. **Apply to Photometric Data**: Use the trained model to reconstruct spectra for new objects from their sparse photometric data.

7. **Uncertainty Quantification**: Calculate uncertainties for each wavelength in the reconstructed spectra.

8. **Outlier Detection**: Analyze the probability distribution of intermediate models to identify peculiar spectral types, detecting outliers efficiently.

This method effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometry and detailed spectral analysis, enabling robust taxonomic classification and outlier detection in large-scale surveys.

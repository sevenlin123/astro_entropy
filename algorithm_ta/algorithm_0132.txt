The algorithm involves training a PCA model on near-IR spectra, performing Bayesian inference in the latent space to reconstruct full spectra from sparse photometry, and propagating uncertainties. Here's the structured algorithm:

1. **Training Phase**:
   - **Step 1**: Gather a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
   - **Step 2**: Preprocess the spectra to ensure they are in a suitable format for PCA analysis.
   - **Step 3**: Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to the preprocessed spectra to derive the principal components (latent space representation).

2. **Determine Optimal Principal Components**:
   - **Step 4**: Use leave-one-out cross-validation to assess the number of principal components that best capture the spectral variability relevant for taxonomic classification and spectral reconstruction. Typically, 4-5 components are found sufficient for classification, while 8-10 enhance reconstruction fidelity.

3. **Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - **Step 5**: For each TNO, input its sparse photometric data into the trained PCA model.
   - **Step 6**: Perform Bayesian inference in the PCA-derived latent space to estimate the latent variables (principal component coefficients) that correspond to the observed photometric data.

4. **Spectral Reconstruction and Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - **Step 7**: Utilize the estimated latent variables to reconstruct the full near-IR spectrum of the TNO.
   - **Step 8**: Quantify and propagate uncertainties from the photometric measurements through the model to determine credible intervals for the reconstructed spectrum.

5. **Applications and Analysis**:
   - **Step 9**: Apply the reconstructed spectra for taxonomic classification, outlier detection, and optimizing JWST/NIRCam filter configurations.
   - **Step 10**: Generate outputs including reconstructed spectra, uncertainty estimates, and classification results for each object, providing insights into the compositional structure of TNOs.

The algorithm describes the core computational method for reconstructing full spectra of trans-Neptunian objects from sparse photometric data using a probabilistic latent-space framework. It combines dimensionality reduction with Bayesian inference in the latent space.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Input Data**: Sparse photometric measurements of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

2. **Training Spectral Libraries**:
   - Use a set of near-infrared spectral libraries (e.g., Lowell Observatory Spectral Energy Distribution Library and Deep Spectral Libraries).
   - Preprocess these libraries to extract representative spectral features.

3. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the spectral libraries to reduce dimensionality.
   - Extract the leading principal components that capture the dominant modes of spectral variability relevant for taxonomic classification (4-5 components) and improved reconstruction fidelity (8-10 components).

4. **Probabilistic Latent-Space Modeling**:
   - Model the spectral manifold of TNOs using Gaussian Processes and Bayesian inference in the reduced latent space.
   - Perform Bayesian inference to reconstruct full spectra from the sparse photometric data while propagating measurement uncertainties.

5. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Use the trained probabilistic model to infer the full spectrum of each TNO from its photometric data.
   - Quantify uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra by sampling from the posterior distribution.

6. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**:
   - Apply the framework to evaluate the information content of different survey instrument configurations (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) for optimal spectral reconstruction.
   - Detect rare spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models from the continuous topological manifold.

### Output:
- Reconstructed full spectra of TNOs with associated uncertainties.
- Optimized survey configurations for future observations.
- Detection and reconstruction of rare spectral types.

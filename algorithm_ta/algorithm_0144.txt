The core reconstruction framework can be broken down into a series of computational steps as follows:

1. **Training Phase**: 
   - **Step 1**: Collect and preprocess a dataset of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
   - **Step 2**: Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on this dataset to reduce its dimensionality. This step identifies the principal components that explain the majority of the spectral variability among the TNOs.

2. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - **Step 3**: Set up a probabilistic model in the reduced PCA space. The model is designed to infer the underlying spectral components based on sparse photometric measurements.
   - **Step 4**: Use Bayesian techniques to propagate measurement uncertainties. This involves quantifying the uncertainties associated with the photometric data and incorporating them into the inference process to obtain reliable estimates of the spectral components.

3. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - **Step 5**: Given the photometric data of a new TNO, the algorithm reconstructs the full spectrum by combining the inferred principal components. The reconstruction process takes into account the uncertainties propagated from the Bayesian inference step.
   - **Step 6**: The algorithm ensures that the reconstructed spectra have empirical credible-interval coverage across all wavelengths, ensuring the reliability of the reconstruction.

4. **Optimization and Validation**:
   - **Step 7**: Apply Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to determine the optimal number of principal components needed for accurate spectral reconstruction. This step helps in balancing the model complexity and performance.
   - **Step 8**: Use the optimized model to perform survey optimization, identifying the most informative filter combinations for future observations to enhance the taxonomic classification of TNOs.

5. **Outlier Detection and Rare Spectral Types**:
   - **Step 9**: Incorporate a mechanism to detect and reconstruct rare spectral types by allowing the model to consider low-probability intermediate models from the continuous topological manifold. This step enhances the model's ability to identify peculiar cases like the Neptune Trojans 2006~RJ$_{103}$ and 2011~SO$_{277}$.

This structured approach ensures that the framework effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral analysis, providing a robust tool for mapping the compositional structure of minor planets in large-scale surveys.

**Computational Algorithm for Spectral Reconstruction of Trans-Neptunian Objects**

1. **Training Set Generation**:
   - Collect a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) with known taxonomic classifications and compositions.
   - Transform each spectrum into its principal component (PC) representation using a technique like Principal Component Analysis (PCA).
   - The number of significant principal components (approx. 4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for higher fidelity) is determined based on the explained variance and cross-validation results.

2. **Forward Modeling**:
   - For each object, input the photometric flux measurements obtained from sparse photometric observations.
   - Map these photometric measurements into the latent space of principal components using a probabilistic model that incorporates measurement uncertainties.
   - The forward model estimates the probability distribution over the latent (principal component) space given the observed photometric data.

3. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - Initialize the prior distribution over the latent space based on the PCA representation of the training set.
   - Compute the likelihood of the observed photometric data given the latent variables using the forward model.
   - Apply Bayes' theorem to derive the posterior distribution over the latent space: \( P(\text{Latent}|\text{Photometry}) = \frac{P(\text{Photometry}|\text{Latent}) \times P(\text{Latent})}{P(\text{Photometry})} \).

4. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Use the posterior distribution of the latent space to reconstruct the original spectral space.
   - This involves mapping the latent variables back to the spectral domain using the inverse transformation of the PCA (e.g., through linear combinations of the principal components).
   - Propagate the uncertainties from the latent space to the reconstructed spectra, ensuring that the empirical credible-interval coverage is achieved (e.g., ~95%).

5. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**:
   - Evaluate the information content of different JWST/NIRCam filter configurations to identify optimal photometric bands for TNO taxonomy.
   - Enhance the detection of rare spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models within the continuous spectral manifold.

This algorithm effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral analysis, enabling robust spectral reconstruction and taxonomic classification of TNOs.

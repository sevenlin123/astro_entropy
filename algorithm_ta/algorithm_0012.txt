The core computational framework revolves around Bayesian inference in a probabilistic latent-space model built using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on a training set of near-IR spectra. Here's the step-by-step breakdown:

1. **Training Phase**:
   - **Input**: Training set of near-IR spectra with their corresponding photometric measurements.
   - **Compute PCA**: Perform Principal Component Analysis on the training spectra to extract the principal components (latent factors). The number of components (k) is determined based on the explained variance, typically capturing ~95% of variance or up to the point where additional components do not significantly improve the model.
   - **Model Construction**: Construct a probabilistic model in the latent space, which includes prior distributions over the latent variables based on the training data.

2. **Reconstruction Phase**:
   - **Input**: Photometric measurements (fluxes) of a new TNO object in sparse observation.
   - **Likelihood Calculation**: For each possible combination of latent factors (principal components), compute the likelihood of observing the given photometric fluxes, considering the measurement uncertainties.
   - **Bayesian Inference**: Use Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) or variational inference to sample from the posterior distribution of the latent factors, conditioned on the observed photometry.
   - **Spectral Reconstruction**: Reconstruct the full spectrum by projecting the sampled latent factors back into the original spectral space. This involves mapping each latent factor vector through the inverse PCA transformation to estimate the original spectrum.
   - **Uncertainty Propagation**: Compute the uncertainty in the reconstructed spectrum by evaluating the variance in the latent factors and propagating it through the inverse transformation.

3. **Outlier Detection**:
   - After reconstructing the spectra, perform statistical tests (e.g., comparing reconstructed vs. expected photometric fluxes) to identify objects whose reconstructed spectra significantly differ from their photometric observations, indicating outliers or unusual spectral types.

4. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Using the trained model, evaluate the information content of different filter configurations by simulating how well they can constrain the latent factors. Choose filter combinations that maximize the ability to reconstruct the latent space accurately, thereby optimizing future observations for TNO taxonomy.

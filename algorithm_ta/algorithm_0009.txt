The core computational algorithm involves several key steps:

1. **Training a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Model**  
   - Train a PCA model on a dataset of near-infrared spectra to capture the principal components representing the dominant modes of spectral variability.

2. **Project Photometric Data into the Latent Space**  
   - Use the trained PCA model to project the sparse photometric measurements of each TNO into the reduced latent space.

3. **Perform Bayesian Inference in the Latent Space**  
   - Conduct Bayesian inference in the PCA-reduced space to estimate the posterior distribution of the latent variables, considering the uncertainties in the photometric measurements.

4. **Reconstruct Full Spectra**  
   - Reconstruct the full near-infrared spectra from the estimated latent variables, incorporating the propagated uncertainties to assess the credibility of the reconstructed spectra.

5. **Determine Optimal Filters for Future Surveys**  
   - Apply the framework to evaluate the information content of different filter combinations to optimize future observations for TNO taxonomy.

6. **Detect and Reconstruct Rare Spectral Types**  
   - Utilize the probabilistic framework to identify and constrain unusual spectral types by allowing exploration of low-probability intermediate models on the spectral manifold.

This method effectively bridges sparse photometry and detailed spectral analysis, enabling robust reconstruction and classification of TNO spectra.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework for TNOs from Sparse Photometry

1. **Input Data**: Sparse photometric measurements of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

2. **Preprocessing**:
   - Normalize the photometric data.
   - Convert photometric measurements into a numerical format suitable for analysis.

3. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Train a PCA model using a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of TNOs.
   - Extract principal components to capture the spectral variability.

4. **Probabilistic Latent-Space Model**:
   - Map the normalized photometric data into the latent space defined by the PCA.
   - Use Bayesian inference to model the relationship between the latent space and the full spectra.

5. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Reconstruct the full spectra from the latent space representation.
   - Propagate measurement uncertainties through the Bayesian model.

6. **Component Selection**:
   - Use leave-one-out cross-validation to determine the optimal number of principal components (4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for improved fidelity).
   - Retain these components for the final spectral reconstruction.

7. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Analyze the information content of JWST/NIRCam filters.
   - Identify optimal filter configurations (e.g., F090W, F115W, F410M, F460M) for future observations.

8. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Apply the framework to detect rare spectral types by constraining photometry.
   - Use the latent space to identify and reconstruct unusual spectral models.

9. **Output**: Reconstructed spectra with uncertainty estimates, optimized survey configurations, and identified outliers.

This structured approach efficiently bridges sparse photometry and spectroscopy, enabling robust analysis of TNO compositions in large-scale surveys.

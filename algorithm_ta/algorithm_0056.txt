The computational algorithm described in the paper focuses on probabilistic spectral reconstruction using sparse photometric data and a pre-trained principal component model. Here is a structured description of the steps involved in implementing this framework:

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction (PSR)**

**Inputs:**
- Sparse photometric measurements of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs)
- Pre-trained Principal Component (PC) model derived from near-infrared (near-IR) spectra
- Bayesian inference setup configured for the reduced PC space
- Filter configurations from JWST/NIRCam for potential survey optimization

**Outputs:**
- Reconstructed full spectra along with uncertainty estimates
- Probabilistic classification of TNO taxonomic types
- Optimized NIRCam filter settings for enhanced survey planning
- Detection of outliers and unique spectral types among TNOs

**Steps:**

1. **Data Collection**: For each TNO, gather its sparse photometric measurements across various astronomical bands.

2. **Spectral Manifold Representation**: Utilize a pre-trained Principal Component Analysis (PCA) model, which has been built from a comprehensive set of near-IR spectra. This model captures the essential variability present in TNO spectral data.

3. **Bayesian Inference in Reduced Space**: Conduct Bayesian inference within the PCA-derived space. This step incorporates the measurement uncertainties inherent in the photometric data, allowing for a probabilistic approach to spectral reconstruction.

4. **Spectral Reconstruction and Uncertainty Quantification**: Using the posterior distributions obtained from Bayesian inference, reconstruct the full spectrum for each TNO. This process also quantifies the uncertainty at each wavelength, providing a measure of confidence in the reconstructed spectrum.

5. **Optimal Filter Configuration**: Assess the information content of each JWST/NIRCam filter configuration. Select those filters that maximize the fidelity of spectral reconstruction and minimize uncertainty, thereby optimizing future surveys for taxonomy studies.

6. **Outlier Detection and Unique Spectral Types**: Implement a constraint-based photometry approach to detect TNOs with unusual spectral features. This involves identifying low-probability intermediate models within the spectral manifold, thus pinpointing rare or peculiar spectral types.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**: Based on the reconstructed spectra and their position within the spectral manifold, classify each TNO into its respective taxonomic category, leveraging the structured spectral variability captured by the PCA model.

This algorithm effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral analysis, offering a robust framework for taxonomic classification, survey optimization, and outlier detection in the study of trans-Neptunian objects.

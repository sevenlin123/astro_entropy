The algorithm involves using principal component analysis (PCA) and Bayesian inference to reconstruct the spectral curve of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric data. Here's a detailed breakdown of the steps:

**Step 1: Preprocessing the Photometric Data**

- **Input:** Sparse photometric measurements of TNOs across various photometric bands.
- **Processing:** Normalize and standardize the photometric data to account for any offsets or scaling factors. This ensures that each band's measurements are comparable and ready for dimensionality reduction techniques.

**Step 2: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on Reference Spectra**

- **Training Set:** Utilize a sample of known near-infrared (near-IR) spectra as the training set.
- **Dimensionality Reduction:** Apply PCA to this training set to identify the principal components (PCs) that capture the majority of the spectral variability. PCs represent the orthogonal directions of maximum variance in the spectral data.
- **Component Selection:** Based on cross-validation, select the optimal number of principal components (4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for improved reconstruction fidelity).

**Step 3: Building the Probabilistic Model in Latent Space**

- **Latent Space Representation:** Map the original spectral data into the PCA-derived latent space. Each point in this space corresponds to a linear combination of the principal components.
- **Probabilistic Framework:** Develop a Bayesian model where each latent variable (principal component scores) has a probability distribution. This allows for the quantification of uncertainties in the latent space.

**Step 4: Bayesian Inference for Posterior Sampling**

- **Model Setup:** Define the likelihood function based on the observed photometric data and the probabilistic model in the latent space. The prior distributions for the latent variables are typically Gaussian, reflecting the assumption of smoothness in the spectral manifold.
- **Sampling Method:** Employ Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods to explore the posterior distribution of the latent variables given the photometric data. MCMC generates a sequence of samples that converge to the posterior distribution.
- **Posterior Distribution:** The samples from the MCMC process represent the possible values of the latent variables, capturing the uncertainty in the spectral reconstruction.

**Step 5: Reconstructing the Spectrum from Posterior Samples**

- **Mapping Back to Spectral Space:** Use the sampled latent variables (from the posterior distribution) to reconstruct the full spectral curves in the original spectral space.
- **Uncertainty Propagation:** Calculate the uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra by analyzing the variability in the posterior samples. This provides credible intervals that indicate the range within which the true spectrum is likely to lie.

**Step 6: Optional - Detection of Rare Spectral Types**

- **Application to Outliers:** For detecting rare spectral types, constrain the photometric data to select low-probability intermediate models from the continuous topological manifold. This step enhances the detection of unusual spectra not captured in the training set.

This structured approach efficiently combines dimensionality reduction with probabilistic modeling and Bayesian inference, enabling robust spectral reconstruction from sparse photometric data.

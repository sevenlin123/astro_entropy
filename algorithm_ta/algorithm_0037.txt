**Algorithm Description: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework for Trans-Neptunian Objects**

**Step 1:** Preprocess the near-infrared spectroscopy data by normalizing and aligning wavelengths to ensure consistency across all observations.

**Step 2:** Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to the preprocessed spectral data to derive a set of principal components that capture the major sources of spectral variability among the TNOs.

**Step 3:** Determine the optimal number of principal components (K) through leave-one-out cross-validation, ensuring that K is sufficient to capture the necessary spectral information for accurate reconstruction.

**Step 4:** Train a probabilistic model in the latent space defined by the first K principal components. This model represents the probability distribution of the latent variables (principal components) given the observed photometric data.

**Step 5:** For each trans-Neptunian object, perform Bayesian inference in the latent space to estimate the posterior distribution of the principal components, incorporating the uncertainties in the photometric measurements.

**Step 6:** Use the estimated posterior distributions to reconstruct the full spectral parameters, effectively mapping the latent space representation back into the original high-dimensional spectral space.

**Step 7:** Propagate the uncertainties from the posterior distributions through the reconstruction process to obtain confidence intervals for the reconstructed spectra.

**Step 8:** Validate the reconstructed spectra against the original data (omitted during training) to assess the accuracy and reliability of the spectral reconstruction.

**Step 9:** Apply the trained framework to new photometric observations of TNOs to predict their spectral properties, enabling taxonomic classification, survey optimization, and outlier detection without requiring full spectroscopy.

**Step 10:** Output the reconstructed spectra along with their associated uncertainties, ready for further analysis and interpretation in planetary science studies.

The computational algorithm follows these steps:

1. Preprocess near-infrared spectral data of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) to create a dataset suitable for training.

2. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the spectral data to reduce it into a lower-dimensional principal component representation. Retain enough components to capture the essential spectral variability (approximately 4-5 for taxonomic classification, up to 10 for improved reconstruction fidelity).

3. Develop a probabilistic latent-space model using the PCA-reduced data. This model captures the structure and correlations inherent in the spectral manifold of TNOs.

4. Implement Bayesian inference within this reduced space to reconstruct full spectra from sparse photometric measurements. This involves propagating measurement uncertainties through the model.

5. Apply the framework to perform spectral reconstruction by inputting photometric data and using the trained model to infer the most probable underlying spectra, considering the latent variables.

6. Use the reconstructed spectra to classify the objects into taxonomic categories based on their spectral features.

7. Optionally, apply the framework for survey optimization by evaluating the information content of different filter combinations, identifying those that maximize the retention of spectral information.

8. The algorithm also includes functionality to detect and reconstruct rare spectral types by allowing the model to explore the continuous manifold of possible spectral solutions and identify outliers based on the probability of intermediate models. 

The algorithm focuses on efficient spectral reconstruction, uncertainty quantification, and application to both classification and survey planning, leveraging the probabilistic framework for robust analysis.

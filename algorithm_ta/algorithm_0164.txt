The computational algorithm for the probabilistic spectral reconstruction framework involves several key steps:

1. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Training**: 
   - Apply PCA to a training set of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra to capture the dominant spectral variability modes. 
   - Retain approximately 4-5 principal components for taxonomic classification and increase to 8-10 for improved spectral reconstruction fidelity and uncertainty calibration.

2. **Spectral Manifold Modeling**:
   - Construct a spectral manifold using the trained PCA representation.
   - Model the spectral manifold to represent the latent space of TNO spectra.

3. **Bayesian Inference in Reduced Space**:
   - Perform Bayesian inference within the reduced PCA space to reconstruct full spectra from sparse photometric data.
   - Propagate measurement uncertainties through the Bayesian framework.

4. **Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Use the constructed spectral manifold and Bayesian posterior distributions to probabilistically reconstruct full spectra.
   - Quantify uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra.

5. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Apply the framework to evaluate the information content of different filter configurations for future missions (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Identify optimal filter combinations that maximize spectral reconstruction fidelity.

6. **Outlier Detection and Rare Spectral Type Reconstruction**:
   - Allow for the identification of photometric data that constrains intermediate models with low probabilities.
   - Reconstruct rare or peculiar spectral types, such as those of Neptune Trojans, by leveraging the continuous topological manifold.

This algorithm bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral analysis, enabling statistically rigorous mapping of the compositional structure of minor planets in large-scale surveys.

**Core Computational Algorithm for Spectral Reconstruction**

1. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Training**: 
   - Train a PCA model using a dataset of near-infrared spectra to capture the spectral variability of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs). This creates a latent space representation of the spectral data.

2. **Bayesian Inference Setup**:
   - Utilize the latent space derived from PCA to perform Bayesian inference. This involves modeling the spectral manifold of TNOs, enabling the reconstruction of full spectra from sparse photometric measurements.

3. **Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Incorporate measurement uncertainties into the Bayesian model, ensuring that uncertainties in photometric data and spectral reconstructions are appropriately propagated.

4. **Optimal Principal Components Selection**:
   - Determine the optimal number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for taxonomic classification and 8-10 for improved spectral reconstruction fidelity) based on the model's performance and information criteria.

5. **Reconstruction of Spectra**:
   - Map sparse photometric data into the pre-trained latent space and perform Bayesian inference to reconstruct the full near-infrared spectra for each object.

6. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Evaluate the information content of NIRCam filters (e.g., F090W, F115W, F410M, F460M) using the reconstructed spectra to identify optimal filter configurations for future TNO observations.

7. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Identify rare or peculiar spectral types by constraining photometric data to select low-probability intermediate models, detecting anomalies on the spectral manifold.

This structured approach efficiently bridges sparse photometric data with detailed spectral information, enabling robust taxonomic classification and outlier detection.

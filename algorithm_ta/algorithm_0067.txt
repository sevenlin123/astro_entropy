The core computational algorithm involves training a model using principal component analysis (PCA) on a dataset of near-infrared spectra. This model captures the spectral variability in a low-dimensional space. When given sparse photometric data, Bayesian inference is performed within this PCA-reduced space to reconstruct the full spectrum, including uncertainty propagation. This allows for efficient spectral reconstruction and outlier detection. 

**Steps:**

1. **Training Phase:**
   - **Input:** Collection of near-IR spectra.
   - **Apply PCA:** Transform the spectra into a set of principal components, capturing the dominant modes of variability.
   - **Model Development:** Establish a probabilistic latent-space model based on the PCA decomposition.

2. **Spectral Reconstruction:**
   - **Input:** Sparse photometric measurements of a TNO.
   - **Bayesian Inference:** Use the pre-trained PCA model to infer the underlying spectral components in the reduced space.
   - **Reconstruct Spectrum:** Translate the inferred components back into a full spectrum.
   - **Uncertainty Propagation:** Estimate uncertainties in the reconstructed spectrum by considering measurement errors and the probabilistic nature of the model.

3. **Survey Optimization:**
   - **Filter Selection:** Evaluate the information content of different filter combinations (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) by assessing their ability to retain spectral information.
   - **Optimal Configuration:** Identify the most informative filter setups for taxonomic studies.

4. **Outlier Detection:**
   - **Anomaly Identification:** By exploring the spectral manifold, detect objects that deviate significantly from typical spectral patterns, such as rare outliers like Neptune Trojans.
   - **Probability Constraints:** Use the probabilistic framework to constrain photometric data and identify low-probability models corresponding to rare spectral types.

This method efficiently bridges sparse photometry and detailed spectral analysis, enabling robust reconstruction and classification of TNO spectra.

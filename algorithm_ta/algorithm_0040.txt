The algorithm described in the paper can be broken down into the following steps:

1. **Preprocess the Photometric Data**: 
   - Normalize or standardize the sparse photometric measurements of the trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

2. **Train a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Model**:
   - Use a sample of near-infrared spectra to construct a PCA representation. This captures the dominant modes of spectral variability, typically identified as 4 to 5 principal components for taxonomic classification and up to 8–10 components for improved spectral reconstruction.

3. **Perform Bayesian Inference in the Latent Space**:
   - In the reduced PCA space, perform Bayesian inference to estimate the latent variables corresponding to the photometric data. This involves propagating measurement uncertainties through the model.

4. **Reconstruct Full Spectra**:
   - Utilize the inferred latent variables from the Bayesian analysis to reconstruct the full near-infrared spectra of the TNOs.

5. **Validation and Cross-Validation**:
   - Apply leave-one-out cross-validation to assess the accuracy of the spectral reconstructions, ensuring the model's reliability and effectiveness in capturing spectral information from sparse photometry.

6. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Analyze the information content of various photometric filters (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters) to identify optimal configurations for future observations, enhancing the framework's applicability for TNO taxonomy.

7. **Outlier Detection and Rare Spectral Types**:
   - Extend the framework to detect and reconstruct rare spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models within the continuous spectral manifold, thereby identifying unusual or peculiar objects like the Neptune Trojans.

This structured approach effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral reconstruction, providing a robust statistical tool for mapping the compositional structure of minor planets in large-scale surveys.

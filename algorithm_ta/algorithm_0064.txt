The core computational algorithm involves a probabilistic framework combining Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Bayesian inference to reconstruct TNO spectra from sparse photometry. Here's a structured overview of the steps:

1. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Train a PCA model on a dataset of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of TNOs.
   - Determine the optimal number of principal components (e.g., 4-5 for classification, 8-10 for accurate reconstruction).

2. **Bayesian Inference in Latent Space:**
   - Represent the spectral data in the reduced PCA space.
   - Perform Bayesian inference to estimate spectral parameters using photometric measurements, incorporating measurement uncertainties.

3. **Spectral Reconstruction:**
   - Reconstruct full spectra from the Bayesian inference results.
   - Propagate measurement uncertainties to assess the reliability of the reconstructed spectra.

This method effectively bridges sparse photometry and spectroscopy, enabling robust spectral analysis and taxonomic classification of TNOs.

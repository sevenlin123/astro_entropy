### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction via Latent Space Inference

1. **Training Phase:**
   - **Input:** Near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of a sample of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
   - **Step 1:** Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the training spectra to obtain a set of principal components (PCs). PCs represent the dominant modes of spectral variability.
   - **Step 2:** Use these PCs to construct a latent-space model that captures the spectral manifold of TNOs. This model is a reduced representation of the original spectral data.
   - **Output:** Trained latent-space model with principal components.

2. **Data Preprocessing:**
   - **Input:** Sparse photometric measurements of TNOs.
   - **Step 1:** Convert photometric data into a format compatible with the latent-space model. This may involve normalization or feature extraction to match the dimensions of the PCA-based model.
   - **Output:** Preprocessed photometric data ready for spectral reconstruction.

3. **Bayesian Inference:**
   - **Step 1:** In the reduced latent space, perform Bayesian inference to estimate the posterior distribution of the principal component coefficients given the photometric data.
   - **Step 2:** Propagate measurement uncertainties from the photometric data through the Bayesian framework to quantify uncertainty in the reconstructed spectra.
   - **Output:** Posterior distributions of the principal component coefficients and associated uncertainties.

4. **Spectral Reconstruction:**
   - **Step 1:** Using the posterior estimates of the principal components, reconstruct the full near-IR spectrum for each TNO.
   - **Step 2:** Quantify the uncertainty in the reconstructed spectra by sampling from the posterior distributions of the principal components.
   - **Output:** Reconstructed spectra and their associated uncertainties.

5. **Dimensionality Consideration:**
   - **Step 1:** Use leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to determine the optimal number of principal components required for accurate spectral reconstruction. Typically, 4-5 PCs are sufficient for taxonomic classification, while 8-10 PCs improve fidelity and uncertainty calibration.
   - **Output:** Optimal number of principal components based on cross-validation.

6. **Survey Optimization:**
   - **Step 1:** Apply the trained framework to evaluate the information content of different filter configurations for future surveys (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).
   - **Step 2:** Identify filter combinations that maximize the ability to reconstruct spectra and detect rare spectral types.
   - **Output:** Optimally informative filter configurations for survey design.

7. **Outlier Detection and Rare Spectral Types:**
   - **Step 1:** Allow the Bayesian framework to constrain photometry to select low-probability intermediate models from the continuous spectral manifold.
   - **Step 2:** Detect outliers or rare spectral types by identifying objects whose photometric data cannot be well-represented by the typical spectral manifold.
   - **Output:** Identified outlier objects with peculiar spectral characteristics.

8. **Final Output:**
   - **Result:** Full near-IR spectra reconstructed from sparse photometry, quantified uncertainties, and identification of rare or unusual spectral types.
   - **Visualization:** Not required as per the problem statement.
   - **Validation:** Ignored as per the problem statement.

The extracted algorithm can be summarized in a step-by-step procedure:

1. Preprocess the spectral data, including normalization and noise reduction.
2. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on a training set of near-infrared spectra to obtain principal components that capture the essential spectral variability.
3. Establish a probabilistic model within the PCA latent space to enable Bayesian inference.
4. For each object under study:
   - Utilize the available photometric measurements to compute the posterior distribution of the latent variables in the PCA space.
   - Employ these posterior distributions to reconstruct the full spectrum, taking into account the measurement uncertainties.
5. Determine the optimal number of principal components through leave-one-out cross-validation, optimizing for either taxonomic classification (~4-5 components) or high-fidelity spectral reconstruction (~8-10 components).
6. Use the trained model to reconstruct spectra for new objects, identifying outliers by examining deviations in their latent representations.
7. Apply the reconstructed spectra for taxonomic classification and optimize survey strategies, focusing on JWST/NIRCam filters to maximize information content for TNO studies.

This algorithm effectively bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral information, enabling robust spectral reconstruction and facilitating scientific advancements in understanding the compositional diversity of trans-Neptunian objects.

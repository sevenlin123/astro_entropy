**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

**Input:**  
- Training set of TNO spectra  
- Photometric data (fluxes and uncertainties) for target object(s)  
- Predefined spectral bands (e.g., NIRCam filters)  

**Output:**  
- Reconstructed spectrum(s) with uncertainties for each object  

**Steps:**  

1. **Preprocessing:**  
   - Normalize and align all training spectra to a common wavelength grid.  

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**  
   - Compute mean spectrum of the training set.  
   - Subtract mean from each spectrum to center the data.  
   - Calculate covariance or correlation matrix of the centered spectra.  
   - Perform eigenvalue decomposition (e.g., compute SVD) to obtain principal components (PCs) and their loadings.  

3. **Bayesian Inference Setup:**  
   - Represent the spectrum \( S \) as \( \mu + \sum(\beta_i \cdot PC_i) \), where \( \mu \) is the mean spectrum, and \( \beta_i \) are coefficients.  
   - Define the forward model relating spectrum to photometric fluxes: \( F = \int(S \cdot R) \, d\lambda \), where \( R \) is the filter response.  
   - Define the likelihood function: \( P(F|S) = \text{Normal}(F; F_{\text{model}}, \sigma^2) \), where \( \sigma \) is flux uncertainty.  
   - Define priors for \( \beta_i \) based on the training set distribution or other constraints.  

4. **Bayesian Inference:**  
   - For each object, use photometric data \( F \) and priors on \( \beta_i \) to perform Bayesian inference (e.g., MCMC sampling).  
   - Obtain posterior distributions of \( \beta_i \) given \( F \).  

5. **Spectrum Reconstruction:**  
   - Compute the posterior mean of \( \beta_i \).  
   - Reconstruct the spectrum \( S_{\hat{}} = \mu + \sum(\text{mean}(\beta_i) \cdot PC_i) \).  

6. **Uncertainty Propagation:**  
   - Compute the variance-covariance matrix of the posterior \( \beta_i \) distributions.  
   - Propagate these uncertainties through the linear combination to obtain the uncertainty in \( S_{\hat{}} \).  

7. **Output:**  
   - For each object, output \( S_{\hat{}} \) and its associated uncertainty spectrum.  

**End.**

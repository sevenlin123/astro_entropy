The computational algorithm for probabilistic spectral reconstruction involves the following steps:

1. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Training**: 
   - Train a PCA model on a comprehensive dataset of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra to capture the primary modes of spectral variability. The optimal number of principal components (e.g., 8-10) is chosen to ensure accurate reconstruction and uncertainty calibration.

2. **Data Preprocessing**:
   - Normalize or standardize the sparse photometric measurements to account for potential biases and ensure compatibility with the PCA model.

3. **Projection into Latent Space**:
   - For each object, project its photometric data into the PCA-derived latent space. This step maps the photometric measurements into the lower-dimensional spectral manifold.

4. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - Perform Bayesian inference within the latent space to estimate the underlying spectral components. This involves defining a prior distribution and updating it based on the observed photometric data, considering measurement uncertainties.

5. **Spectrum Reconstruction**:
   - Using the posterior distributions from the Bayesian inference, reconstruct the full spectrum by mapping the latent space estimates back to the original spectral space.

6. **Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Calculate the uncertainty intervals for the reconstructed spectra, ensuring that the credible-interval coverage is approximately 95%.

This structured approach efficiently bridges sparse photometric data with detailed spectral information, enabling robust reconstruction and analysis of trans-Neptunian object spectra.

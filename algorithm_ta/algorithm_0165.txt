**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Input Data**: Sparse photometric measurements of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

2. **Preprocessing**:
   - Normalize photometric data to account for measurement uncertainties.
   - Convert photometric measurements into a common spectral resolution.

3. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Train PCA on a sample of near-infrared (near-IR) spectra of TNOs.
   - Extract principal components (PCs) to represent the spectral manifold.

4. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - Represent the spectral manifold using the extracted PCs.
   - Perform Bayesian inference in the reduced PCA space to reconstruct full spectra from the sparse photometric data.
   - Propagate measurement uncertainties through the Bayesian model.

5. **Model Selection**:
   - Use LOOCV to determine the optimal number of principal components for spectral reconstruction (typically 4–5 for taxonomic classification and 8–10 for higher fidelity).
   - Select the number of components that provide the best balance between model complexity and spectral reconstruction accuracy.

6. **Spectral Reconstruction**:
   - Project the photometric data onto the selected PCA basis.
   - Reconstruct the full near-IR spectrum using the Bayesian model, incorporating prior distributions based on the training data.
   - Quantify uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra.

7. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Analyze the probability distribution of intermediate models in the PCA space.
   - Identify and reconstruct rare spectral types by constraining photometry to select low-probability intermediate models.
   - Detect peculiar objects like Neptune Trojans by their deviation from the typical spectral manifold.

8. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Apply the framework to assess the information content of different JWST/NIRCam filters.
   - Determine optimal filter configurations for future observations to maximize the retention of spectral information for TNO taxonomy.

9. **Output**:
   - Reconstructed full near-IR spectra for each object.
   - Uncertainty estimates for each wavelength.
   - Optimally recommended filter configurations for survey design.

This algorithm bridges the gap between sparse photometric data and detailed spectral information, enabling accurate taxonomic classification and efficient survey planning for minor planets.

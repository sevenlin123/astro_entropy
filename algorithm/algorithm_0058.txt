The algorithm systematically processes sparse photometric data to reconstruct spectral properties of trans-Neptunian objects through Bayesian inference. It initializes a hierarchical model linking data to spectral parameters, iteratively refines parameter estimates using MCMC sampling, and performs posterior analysis for taxonomic classification, outlier detection, and survey evaluation.

**Algorithm:**

1. **Input:** Sparse photometric measurements, observation details, prior distributions of spectral reflectance, and albedo parameters.

2. **Initialization:** 
   - Establish Bayesian hierarchical model linking photometric data to spectral parameters.
   - Define forward model simulating light interaction with the object's surface and atmosphere.

3. **Parameter Estimation:**
   - For each object, propose initial spectral parameter values based on prior distributions.
   - Use the forward model to simulate expected light curves based on proposed parameters.
   - Calculate the likelihood of the observed data under these parameters.
   - Apply Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling to explore the posterior distribution of parameters, updating them iteratively to maximize data consistency.

4. **Convergence Check:**
   - Diagnose MCMC chains to ensure convergence, typically using methods like Gelman-Rubin statistic or visual inspection of trace plots.

5. **Posterior Analysis:**
   - Extract credible intervals for spectral reflectance and albedo parameters to assess uncertainty.
   - Classify objects into spectral taxonomies by comparing posterior probabilities across categories.
   - Evaluate survey strategy effectiveness by analyzing parameter recovery uncertainties.
   - Identify outliers based on discrepancies between posterior distributions and expected patterns.

6. **Output:**
   - Probabilistic reconstructions of spectral parameters.
   - Taxonomic classifications of each object.
   - Flags indicating outliers.
   - Metrics assessing the efficiency and optimization potential of the survey strategy.

This structured approach efficiently reconstructs spectral properties even from limited data, providing robust classifications and insights for astronomical surveys.

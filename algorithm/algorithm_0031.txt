1. Initialize the spectral parameters, taxonomic classification labels, and hyperparameters of the Dirichlet process mixture model based on prior knowledge and sparse photometric data.

2. Set up the Bayesian model to jointly infer the spectral energy distribution (SED) and taxonomic classification of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).

3. Use a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method with an Adaptive Metropolis Hastings (AMH) sampler to explore the joint posterior distribution of the spectral parameters, classification labels, and mixture model hyperparameters.

4. Iteratively update the spectral parameters by sampling from their posterior distributions given the current classification labels and photometric data.

5. Update the taxonomic classification labels by determining the most probable class for each object based on the current mixture model and observed photometry.

6. Adjust the hyperparameters of the Dirichlet process mixture model to better represent the underlying population structure as informed by the data.

7. Quantify uncertainties in the reconstructed spectra and classification probabilities by examining the posterior distributions.

8. Check for convergence of the MCMC chains and iterative improvements in parameter estimates.

9. Stop the algorithm when convergence criteria are met, retaining the final inferred SEDs, taxonomic classifications, and associated uncertainties.

This describes the core computational algorithm for probabilistic spectral reconstruction and taxonomic classification of trans-Neptunian objects.

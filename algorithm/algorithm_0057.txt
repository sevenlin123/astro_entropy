1. **Input Data Preparation**: Start by collecting sparse multi-color photometric data of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs). Ensure the data is standardized and normalized to handle variations in observation conditions.

2. **Bayesian Model Setup**: Define a Bayesian statistical model that includes prior distributions based on empirical color-color relations derived from known TNO SEDs. These priors constrain the possible parameter values, incorporating physical knowledge about TNO spectra.

3. **Forward Model Implementation**: Develop a forward model that simulates the transformation of spectral energy distributions (SEDs) into observed photometric colors. This model acts as a bridge between the theoretical SEDs and the observed photometric data.

4. **Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) Sampling**: Utilize an MCMC algorithm to explore the parameter space. The MCMC sampler generates posterior distributions of SED parameters by combining the observed photometric data with the empirical priors, ensuring consistency across all data points.

5. **Posterior Analysis**: After obtaining the posterior distributions, analyze them to quantify uncertainties and correlations among parameters such as albedo, spectral slopes, and others. This analysis provides a comprehensive understanding of the parameter relationships and their reliability.

6. **Outlier Detection**: Apply statistical methods to the posterior distributions to identify outlier objects. These outliers are flagged based on discrepancies between their posterior probabilities and the expected distribution, indicating potential misclassifications or unusual characteristics.

7. **Output Generation**: Compile the results into reconstructed SEDs complete with uncertainty estimates and outlier flags. This output is used for further analysis, taxonomy, survey optimization, and identifying anomalies in the dataset.

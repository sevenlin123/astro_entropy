The core computational algorithm for the probabilistic spectral reconstruction of trans-Neptunian objects from sparse photometry involves the following steps:

1. **Initialize Gaussian Process Models**: For each TNO, create a Gaussian Process model where the spectral parameters (like temperature and grain size) are treated as random variables. The covariance matrix is determined by the physical properties of the materials on the object's surface.

2. **Set Up Hierarchical Probabilistic Model**: Implement a hierarchical model with an empirical prior distribution of spectral parameters. This prior is informed by known TNOs, providing a foundation before updating with observed data.

3. **Conduct MCMC Sampling with Nested Acceleration**: Use a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method, enhanced with nested sampling, to efficiently explore the parameter space. This acceleration balances computation time and accuracy while sampling the posterior distributions.

4. **Reconstruct Spectral Energy Distribution**: From the posterior distributions obtained via MCMC, determine the most probable SED for each TNO, quantifying uncertainties and identifying outliers in the data.

This algorithm efficiently reconstructs the SED of TNOs from sparse photometric data, enabling taxonomic classification, survey optimization, and outlier detection.

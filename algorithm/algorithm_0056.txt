The algorithm is designed to reconstruct the spectral energy distribution (SED) of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) from sparse photometric data. Here's the step-by-step algorithm:

1. **Preprocess the Input Data:**
   - Convert the raw photometric data into unit flux measurements across all available bands.
   - Normalize each band's flux to ensure consistency.

2. **Initialize Possible SED Models:**
   - For each TNO, generate a set of potential SED models.
   - Each SED is modeled as a piecewise power law with varying exponents and breakpoints.
   - Define the number of segments, exponents, and break points for each piecewise power law.

3. **Compute the Likelihood of Each SED Model:**
   - Use Bayesian probability theory to calculate the likelihood of each candidate SED given the observed photometric data.
   - Incorporate prior knowledge about typical albedos and sizes of TNOs to inform the priors.

4. **Perform Bayesian Inference Using MCMC:**
   - Apply a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling algorithm to explore the posterior distribution of the SED parameters.
   - Use the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm or another suitable MCMC method to sample the parameter space.
   - Determine the posterior probabilities of the SEDs based on the sampled distributions.

5. **Rank the Reconstructed SEDs:**
   - Order the SEDs based on their posterior probabilities.
   - Identify the most probable SED as the one with the highest posterior probability.

6. **Output the Results:**
   - Provide the ranked list of SEDs, including uncertainties and correlations between photometric bands.
   - Include the most probable SED and its associated confidence intervals.
   - Output any additional statistics or metrics derived from the posterior distributions.

This algorithm effectively reconstructs the SEDs of TNOs, even when faced with sparse and noisy photometric data, providing a robust framework for subsequent taxonomic studies, survey optimization, and outlier detection.

### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. **Initialize Principal Component Analysis (PCA):**
   - Train PCA on a reference set of TNO spectra to define a latent space.
   - Extract the top K principal components, where K is determined based on variance explained (~4-5 for taxonomic classification, ~8-10 for high fidelity reconstruction).

2. **Generate Synthetic Training Data:**
   - Project the reference spectra onto the PCA latent space to obtain latent coefficients.
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to smooth and expand the latent distribution, creating a continuous manifold of possible spectral states.

3. **Simulate Noisy Photometry:**
   - For each augmented spectrum, compute the expected photometric measurements by convolving with instrument-specific filter curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Add Gaussian noise with a relative uncertainty of ~20% to simulate realistic observational uncertainties.

4. **Train AutoML Regressor (Encoder):**
   - Use the noisy photometric measurements as input and train an ensemble model (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to predict latent coefficients from photometry.

5. **Define Taxonomic Classifier:**
   - Train another AutoML classifier on the latent space to predict taxonomic labels (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organic) based on the latent coefficients.

6. **Inference Pipeline:**
   - For a new photometric observation:
     - Pass the sparse photometry through the trained encoder to obtain a posterior distribution over latent coefficients.
     - Decode the latent coefficients using the inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the full spectrum.
     - Apply the taxonomic classifier to determine the most probable spectral type.

7. **Validation and Metrics:**
   - Perform LOOCV to assess reconstruction accuracy and empirical credible-interval coverage.
   - Compute metrics including reconstruction error, variability explained, and taxonomic classification accuracy.

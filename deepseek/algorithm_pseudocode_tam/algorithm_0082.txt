Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
- Seed TNO spectra (training set)
- NIRCam filter transmission curves
- Photometric observations (sparse)

Output:
- Reconstructed spectra
- Taxonomic classification probabilities

Steps:

1. Preprocessing:
   a. Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to the seed TNO spectra to extract principal components (PCs).
   b. Project each seed spectrum onto the PCA latent space to obtain latent coefficients.
   
2. Manifold Augmentation:
   a. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth probability distribution over the latent coefficients.
   b. Generate synthetic spectra by sampling the latent space according to the KDE distribution.
   c. Convolve each synthetic spectrum with NIRCam filter curves to compute synthetic photometry.
   
3. Training Database Construction:
   a. Combine synthetic photometry and corresponding latent coefficients into training instances.
   b. Introduce Gaussian noise (20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric uncertainties.
   
4. AutoML Regression:
   a. Train an ensemble of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) using the training database.
   b. Input: Sparse photometric measurements.
   c. Output: Predicted latent coefficients.
   
5. Posterior Reconstruction:
   a. Apply inverse PCA transform to the predicted latent coefficients to obtain full spectra.
   b. Use the reconstructed spectra for further analysis.
   
6. Taxonomic Classification:
   a. Train a secondary classifier on the latent manifold to predict taxonomic labels.
   b. Classify new objects based on their latent coefficients.
   
7. Validation and Evaluation:
   a. Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) on the original spectra.
   b. Compute RMSE, empirical credible interval coverage, and classification accuracy.
   
8. Applications:
   a. Identify optimal NIRCam filter configurations for survey design.
   b. Detect outliers in the latent space for unusual spectral types.
   
End Algorithm

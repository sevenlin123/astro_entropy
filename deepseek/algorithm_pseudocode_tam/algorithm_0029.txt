**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Prepare Training Data**:
   - Collect a sample of reference TNO spectra.
   - Exclude rare spectral types to focus on dominant classes.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Project the reference spectra onto a PCA basis to define a low-dimensional latent space.
   - Determine the number of principal components (K) that best capture spectral variability (e.g., 4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for reconstruction).

3. **Data Augmentation**:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to generate synthetic latent coefficients, ensuring a smooth and continuous manifold.
   - Adjust KDE bandwidth (e.g., h ≈ 0.5) to balance local fidelity and global smoothness.

4. **Generate Synthetic Photometry**:
   - Convolve augmented spectra with instrument-specific filter curves (e.g., JWST NIRCam) to simulate observed photometric magnitudes.
   - Inject Gaussian noise (e.g., 20% uncertainty) to account for observational uncertainties.

5. **Train AutoML Regressor**:
   - Use an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees to regress from sparse photometry to latent coefficients.
   - AutoML handles feature selection and model optimization.

6. **Inverse PCA Transform**:
   - Apply the inverse PCA transformation to the predicted latent coefficients to reconstruct the full spectral energy distribution.

7. **Validation**:
   - Perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to assess RMSE of latent and spectral reconstructions.
   - Compute empirical credible interval coverage to validate uncertainty quantification.

8. **Survey Optimization**:
   - Evaluate different filter configurations to identify the most informative combinations for spectral reconstruction.

9. **Outlier Detection**:
   - Flag objects with low probability in the latent space or high posterior uncertainty as potential outliers.

**End of Algorithm**

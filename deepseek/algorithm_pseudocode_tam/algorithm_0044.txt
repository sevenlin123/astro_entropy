Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

1. Initialize:
   - Load reference TNO spectra and exclude outliers to form the training set.
   - Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the training spectra to define a low-dimensional latent space.

2. Prepare the Prior:
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients to create a smooth probability distribution representing valid spectral morphologies.

3. Generate Synthetic Photometry:
   - Convolve each spectrum with the instrument's filter curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam) to compute theoretical reflectance.
   - Add Gaussian noise (20% uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric observations.

4. Train the Encoder:
   - Use AutoML (e.g., AutoGluon) to train a regressor mapping noisy photometry to latent coefficients.
   - The regressor is an ensemble of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees.

5. Validate the Model:
   - Perform Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) on the training set.
   - For each test spectrum, remove it from the training set, augment the latent space, retrain the encoder, and predict latent coefficients.
   - Reconstruct the spectrum using the inverse PCA transform and compare with the original.

6. Taxonomic Classification:
   - Train a secondary classifier on the latent space to predict spectral types (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, Organics).
   - Output probability distributions for each class.

7. Detect Outliers:
   - Analyze the posterior distribution of latent coefficients.
   - Identify objects with high posterior probabilities in the tails as outliers.

8. Quantify Information Content:
   - Evaluate the sensitivity of spectral reconstruction to different photometric bands.
   - Determine the optimal filter configurations for maximizing spectral reconstruction fidelity.

9. Output:
   - For each object, provide:
     - Reconstructed spectrum with credible intervals.
     - Probabilistic taxonomic classification.
     - Identification of outliers.
     - Optimally informative filter configurations.

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Initialize Training Data**
   - Gather a reference set of 51 TNO near-infrared spectra.
   - Exclude rare spectral types and outliers to focus on dominant taxonomic classes.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
   - Perform PCA on the reference spectra to derive principal components (PCs).
   - Determine the number of PCs (K ≈ 4-10) based on reconstruction fidelity and cross-validation results.

3. **Synthetic Data Augmentation**
   - Project the reference spectra onto the PCA latent space.
   - Apply Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) with a bandwidth of ~0.5 to generate a smooth probability distribution in the latent space.
   - Sample thousands of synthetic latent vectors to augment the training data.

4. **Forward Modeling of Photometry**
   - For each synthetic latent vector, generate forward photometric measurements by convolving the reconstructed spectra with the instrument's filter curves (e.g., JWST/NIRCam).
   - Inject Gaussian noise (20% relative uncertainty) into the synthetic photometry to simulate real-world observational noise.

5. **Training the AutoML Regressor**
   - Use an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees (AutoGluon) to regress the synthetic photometry onto the PCA latent coefficients.
   - The regressor is trained on the augmented dataset to map sparse photometry to latent space coordinates.

6. **LOOCV Validation**
   - For each held-out spectrum, remove it from the training set and retrain the regressor.
   - Predict latent coefficients for the held-out photometry and compare to true values to compute RMSE and credible interval coverage.

7. **Reconstruct Spectra**
   - Transform the predicted latent coefficients back to the full spectrum using the inverse PCA transformation.
   - Ensure the reconstructed spectra are smooth and physically plausible, inheriting the characteristics of the training population.

8. **Taxonomic Classification**
   - Train a secondary classifier on the PCA latent space to predict spectral type probabilities for new observations.
   - Classify objects based on their latent coefficients, providing a probabilistic taxonomic assignment.

9. **Outlier Detection**
   - Identify objects whose reconstructed spectra deviate significantly from the training manifold.
   - Detect rare spectral types or unusual objects requiring follow-up spectroscopy.

10. **Survey Optimization**
    - Simulate various NIRCam filter configurations.
    - Evaluate RMSE and credible interval coverage to determine optimal filter setups for future surveys.

**End Algorithm**

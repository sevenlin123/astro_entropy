**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

**Step-by-Step Computational Steps:**

1. **Prepare Reference Spectra**: Collect and preprocess the reference set of TNO spectra, ensuring they are normalized and formatted appropriately.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**:
   - Compute the PCA of the reference spectra to extract principal component (PC) coefficients and eigenvectors.
   - Determine the number of significant PCs (4-5 for classification, 8-10 for detailed reconstruction).

3. **Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) for Latent Space**:
   - Apply non-parametric KDE to the PC coefficients of the reference spectra to construct a smooth probability distribution in the latent space.

4. **Synthetic Photometry Generation**:
   - For each synthetic latent spectrum, convolve it with the instrument's (e.g., JWST NIRCam) filter functions to compute the expected reflectance in each filter band.

5. **Noise Injection**:
   - Add Gaussian noise with a 20% relative uncertainty to the synthetic photometry to simulate realistic observational uncertainties.

6. **AutoML Regressor Training**:
   - Train an ensemble AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees) to map the sparse photometry (band fluxes) to the latent PCA coefficients.

7. **Predict Latent Coefficients**:
   - Use the trained regressor to predict latent coefficients from new photometric observations.

8. **Reconstruct Full Spectra**:
   - Apply the inverse PCA transformation to the predicted latent coefficients to obtain the full spectral representation.

9. **Output and Uncertainty Propagation**:
   - Output the reconstructed spectrum along with propagated uncertainties derived from the posterior distribution of latent coefficients.

**End of Algorithm**

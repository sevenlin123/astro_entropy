Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
- Observed photometric measurements m (sparse)
- Pre-trained Principal Component (PC) basis derived from reference spectra
- Known uncertainty distribution of photometric measurements

Output:
- Reconstructed spectrum S
- Posterior probabilities over spectral taxonomic classes

Steps:

1. Define the spectral manifold:
   a. Perform PCA on a training set of reference spectra to derive principal components (latent space).
   
2. Establish the prior distribution:
   a. Project reference spectra onto the latent space to get initial coefficients.
   b. Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to model the distribution of these coefficients.
   
3. Generate synthetic photometry:
   a. Convolve reference spectra with NIRCam filter transmission curves.
   b. Add Gaussian noise (20% relative uncertainty) to simulate realistic photometric measurements.
   
4. Train the regressor:
   a. Use AutoML (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) to regress from synthetic photometry to latent coefficients.
   
5. Perform Bayesian inference:
   a. For new photometric observations m:
      i. Predict latent coefficients using the trained regressor.
      ii. Propagate uncertainties through the regressor.
      iii. Apply inverse PCA transform to obtain the reconstructed spectrum S.
      
6. Classify spectral type:
   a. Project latent coefficients onto a trained classifier.
   b. Obtain posterior probabilities over taxonomic classes.
   
7. Validate reconstruction:
   a. Compare reconstructed spectrum S with real spectrum (if available).
   b. Calculate RMSE and credible interval coverage.
   
8. Detect outliers:
   a. Assess posterior probabilities in latent space.
   b. Identify objects with probabilities significantly deviating from the manifold.
   
End.

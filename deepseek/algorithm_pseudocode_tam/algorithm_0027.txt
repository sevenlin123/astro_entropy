**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

1. **Initialize Training Data**
   - Load reference spectra of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs).
   - Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to decompose the spectra into principal components.
   - Project the reference spectra onto the PCA basis to obtain latent coefficients.

2. **Augment Latent Space**
   - Use Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to create a smooth probability distribution of the latent coefficients.
   - Generate synthetic latent coefficients by sampling from the KDE distribution, expanding the training dataset.

3. **Generate Synthetic Photometry**
   - For each synthetic latent coefficient:
     - Convolve the corresponding spectrum with the instrument's filter curves (e.g., JWST NIRCam) to compute expected photometric magnitudes.
     - Add Gaussian noise with 20% relative uncertainty to simulate real-world photometric measurements.

4. **Train AutoML Regressor**
   - Use AutoGluon to train an ensemble of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) that maps noisy photometric measurements to latent coefficients.
   - The regressor is trained on pairs of (synthetic photometry, latent coefficients).

5. **Predict Latent Coefficients**
   - For new photometric observations, pass them through the trained regressor to obtain predicted latent coefficients.

6. **Reconstruct Full Spectrum**
   - Apply the inverse PCA transformation to the predicted latent coefficients to obtain the reconstructed high-resolution spectrum.

7. **Classify Spectral Type (Optional)**
   - Train a separate AutoML classifier on the latent coefficients to predict the spectral type (e.g., Water-type, CO₂-type, Organics-type).
   - Use the predicted latent coefficients to classify the spectral type.

**End Algorithm**

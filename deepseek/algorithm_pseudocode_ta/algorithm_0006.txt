**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry**

**Training Phase:**
1. **Collect Reference Spectra**: Gather a dataset of near-IR spectra for trans-Neptunian objects.
2. **Preprocess Spectra**: Normalize and align the spectra to ensure consistent units and wavelengths.
3. **Compute Principal Components (PCA)**:
   - Apply PCA to the preprocessed spectra to obtain principal components (latent space representation).
   - Retain the top K principal components (e.g., K = 4-5 for taxonomic classification, 8-10 for high-fidelity reconstruction).
4. **Store Loadings and Components**: Save the principal component loadings and eigenvalues for subsequent use.

**Inference Phase:**
1. **Input Sparse Photometry**: For a new object, obtain its sparse photometric measurements across selected bands.
2. **Map Photometry to PCA Space**:
   - Use a precomputed mapping (e.g., linear regression) to project photometric data into the PCA latent space.
   - This step involves estimating the PCA coefficients that best predict the observed photometric fluxes.
3. **Bayesian Inference**:
   - Define a probabilistic model where the PCA coefficients are parameters with Gaussian priors.
   - The likelihood is derived from the relationship between PCA coefficients and photometric measurements.
   - Utilize MCMC or another sampling method to draw posterior samples of the PCA coefficients.
4. **Reconstruct Full Spectrum**:
   - Multiply the posterior mean of the PCA coefficients by the corresponding loadings.
   - Sum the products across all retained PCA components to obtain the reconstructed spectrum.
5. **Propagate Uncertainties**:
   - Calculate the covariance matrix of the posterior PCA coefficients.
   - Apply the covariance matrix to the reconstructed spectrum to determine uncertainty propagation.

**End Algorithm**

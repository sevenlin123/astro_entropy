**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework**

**Step 1: Preprocess the Training Data**
- Input: Collection of near-IR spectra from a sample of TNOs.
- Perform normalization and alignment of spectra to ensure consistency.
- Remove noise and outliers from the training dataset using robust statistical methods.
- Store preprocessed spectra in a matrix format, where each row represents a spectrum and columns represent wavelengths.

**Step 2: Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
- Compute the covariance matrix of the preprocessed spectra.
- Perform eigenvalue decomposition to obtain eigenvectors and eigenvalues.
- Select the top K principal components based on explained variance or domain knowledge (K ranges from 4 to 10 for different applications).
- Construct the reduced-dimensional basis using these selected principal components.

**Step 3: Bayesian Inference Setup**
- Define the probabilistic model in the reduced PCA space.
- Set prior distributions for the latent variables (e.g., mean and variance parameters of the PCA components).
- Define the likelihood function that relates the reduced spectral manifold to the observed photometric measurements.
- Incorporate measurement uncertainties into the likelihood function.

**Step 4: Perform Bayesian Inference**
- Use Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) or variational inference to sample the posterior distribution of the latent variables.
- Propagate measurement uncertainties through the inference process.
- Obtain posterior estimates of the PCA coefficients and their uncertainties.

**Step 5: Reconstruct Full Spectra**
- For each TNO, project the inferred PCA coefficients back into the original spectral space.
- Reconstruct the full spectrum by combining the PCA basis vectors weighted by the inferred coefficients.
- Ensure the reconstructed spectrum aligns with the observed photometric data within the propagated uncertainties.

**Step 6: Uncertainty Calibration**
- Quantify the uncertainty in the reconstructed spectra by calculating the standard deviation of the posterior distribution of the PCA coefficients.
- Assess the credibility of the reconstructed spectra by checking if the observed data falls within the credible intervals.
- Adjust the reconstruction if discrepancies are found, iterating until convergence.

**Step 7: Application to Individual Objects**
- For each TNO, apply the framework by inputting its photometric measurements.
- Repeat Steps 4 to 6 for each object to obtain individualized spectral reconstructions.
- Use the reconstructed spectra for further applications like taxonomic classification or survey optimization.

**Step 8: Outlier Detection and Rare Spectral Types**
- Identify objects with photometric measurements that significantly deviate from the reconstructed spectral manifold.
- Apply additional constraining photometry to refine the reconstruction of outliers.
- Utilize the continuous topological manifold to detect and constrain rare spectral types by exploring low-probability intermediate models.

**End of Algorithm**

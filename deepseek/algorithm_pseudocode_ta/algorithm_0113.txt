```
Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
- Photometric data (sparse measurements) for TNOs
- Associated measurement uncertainties
- Pre-trained Principal Component (PC) basis derived from a sample of near-IR spectra

Output:
- Reconstructed full near-IR spectra
- Uncertainty estimates for each wavelength

Steps:

1. Initialize the principal components (PCs):
   - Load the pre-trained PC basis which captures the spectral variability of TNOs.
   - Number of PCs used determined by previous analysis (e.g., 4-10 PCs depending on application).

2. Project photometric data into the spectral manifold:
   - Map sparse photometric measurements into the latent space defined by the PCs.
   - This involves solving for the linear combination of PCs that best represents the observed photometry.

3. Bayesian inference in the latent space:
   - Formulate a probabilistic model where the latent variables (coefficients of PCs) are inferred using Bayesian methods.
   - The model accounts for the uncertainty in photometric measurements and the structure of the spectral manifold.
   - Use MCMC or variational methods to sample the posterior distribution of the latent variables.

4. Reconstruct the full spectrum:
   - Using the inferred latent variables (PC coefficients), compute the full spectrum by projecting back onto the original spectral space.
   - Combine contributions from all PCs to form the final reconstructed spectrum.

5. Calculate uncertainties for the reconstructed spectrum:
   - Propagate the uncertainties from the latent space (posterior variances of PC coefficients) to the full spectrum.
   - This provides wavelength-dependent uncertainty estimates for the reconstructed spectrum.
```

This algorithm focuses on the core computational steps without validation or optimization procedures, utilizing a probabilistic approach combined with dimensionality reduction to reconstruct spectra from sparse photometric data.

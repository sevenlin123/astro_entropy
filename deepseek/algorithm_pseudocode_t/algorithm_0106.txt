Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework

Input: Sparse photometric data of trans-Neptunian objects

Output: Robust probability density function (PDF) of the object's spectrum

1. Preprocessing:
   a. Retrieve sparse photometric data for the target object.
   b. Correct the data for telluric absorption and instrumental effects.

2. Feature Detection:
   a. Apply a peak detection algorithm to identify emission lines in the spectrum.
   b. Set a noise threshold to filter out insignificant peaks.
   c. Record the positions and fluxes of detected emission lines.

3. Probabilistic Modeling:
   a. For each detected emission line:
      i. Define a probability distribution for flux, width, and position.
      ii. Incorporate observed data and prior knowledge about spectral features.
      iii. Use Bayesian inference to update the probability distribution based on evidence from the data.

   b. Combine individual probability distributions using Bayesian inference to construct the overall spectral PDF.

4. Reconstruct Spectrum:
   a. Perform Monte Carlo sampling of the overall spectral PDF.
   b. Generate multiple possible spectrum realizations.
   c. Compute the mean spectrum from the samples.
   d. Determine confidence intervals based on the variability in the samples.

5. Outlier Detection:
   a. Compare observed photometric measurements with the reconstructed spectra.
   b. Identify discrepancies that exceed predefined thresholds.
   c. Flag objects as potential outliers if significant discrepancies are found.

6. Classification:
   a. Extract spectral features from the reconstructed PDFs.
   b. Use clustering or machine learning techniques to classify objects into spectral taxonomies.

7. Survey Optimization:
   a. Analyze the uncertainties and variances in the reconstructed PDFs.
   b. Optimize future survey configurations by adjusting instrument parameters and exposure times to minimize expected uncertainties.

End of Algorithm

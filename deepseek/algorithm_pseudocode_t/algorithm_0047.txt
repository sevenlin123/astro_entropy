The key steps involve preprocessing the data, setting up a Gaussian Process (GP) model, performing Bayesian inference using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC), and postprocessing to obtain the reconstructed SEDs.

```pseudocode
Algorithm: Probabilistic SED Reconstruction for TNOs

1. Input: Sparse multi-band photometric data and orbital parameters for each object.

2. Preprocessing:
   a. Normalize flux measurements to account for exposure times and telescope characteristics.
   b. Compute variability metrics such as maximum, minimum, and range of flux variations across bands.

   a. Choose a covariance function (e.g., squared exponential) with parameters to estimate.
   b. Define the mean function based on prior knowledge (e.g., constant mean or a trend).
   c. Incorporate orbital parameters into the GP to capture their influence on the SED.

   a. Set up the likelihood function based on the data and GP model.
   b. Define prior distributions for all hyperparameters.
   c. Use HMC to sample from the posterior distribution of the SED parameters.

5. Postprocessing:
   a. Compute point estimates (e.g., median) and credible intervals for the SED.
   b. Identify outliers based on deviations from expected distributions.

6. Output:
   a. Reconstructed SEDs with uncertainties.
   b. Probabilistic classifications and outlier flags for each object.

End of Algorithm
```

### Explanation

The provided algorithm outlines the core steps for reconstructing the spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) using sparse photometric data. Here's a detailed explanation of each step:

1. **Input**: The algorithm starts by taking sparse multi-band photometric data and orbital parameters for each TNO. This data includes flux measurements in different bands and parameters like inclination, eccentricity, and orbital period.

2. **Preprocessing**:
   - **Normalization**: Flux measurements are normalized to adjust for exposure times and telescope characteristics, ensuring that measurements from different instruments or times can be compared.
   - **Variability Metrics**: Variability metrics such as maximum, minimum, and range of flux variations are computed to capture the photometric variability of the object.
   - **Orbital Parameters**: These include inclination, eccentricity, and orbital period, which are crucial for understanding the object's properties and its light curve.

3. **Define the Gaussian Process (GP) Model**:
   - **Covariance Function**: A suitable covariance function (e.g., squared exponential) is chosen, which determines the smoothness and correlation structure of the SED.
   - **Mean Function**: A mean function is defined, often based on prior knowledge, to serve as a baseline for the SED.
   - **Incorporation of Orbital Parameters**: These parameters are integrated into the GP to account for their influence on the SED, enhancing the model's accuracy.

4. **Bayesian Inference using HMC**:
   - **Likelihood Function**: Defined based on the data and GP model, it quantifies the probability of observing the data given the model parameters.
   - **Prior Distributions**: Prior beliefs about the hyperparameters are specified, reflecting existing knowledge before seeing the data.
   - **HMC Sampling**: Hamiltonian Monte Carlo is employed to draw samples from the posterior distribution, providing estimates of the SED parameters.

5. **Postprocessing**:
   - **Point Estimates and Credible Intervals**: From the posterior samples, point estimates (like the median) and credible intervals are computed to summarize the reconstructed SED.
   - **Outlier Detection**: Objects are classified as outliers if their observed data deviate significantly from the expected distributions derived from the posterior samples.

6. **Output**:
   - **Reconstructed SEDs**: The algorithm outputs the reconstructed SEDs along with their uncertainties, providing a comprehensive view of the object's spectral characteristics.
   - **Probabilistic Classifications**: Objects are classified based on their SEDs, aiding in taxonomic grouping and identifying outliers.

This algorithm effectively combines preprocessing, model setup, Bayesian inference, and postprocessing to provide accurate and robust SED reconstructions for TNOs, leveraging sparse photometric data and orbital information.

**Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction Framework**

1. **Input:** Sparse photometric observations of TNOs (flux measurements in multiple bands), known spectral templates.

2. **Initialize:** 
   - Set up the Bayesian Mixture of Experts (B-MoE) model.
   - Each expert corresponds to a spectral template; define prior distributions for model parameters.
   - Define hyperparameters for the hierarchical Bayesian structure.

3. **Training (B-MoE Model):**
   - For each spectral template, establish its contribution as an expert in the mixture.
   - Train the B-MoE model using the sparse photometric data.
   - Update mixing weights and spectral component probabilities via Bayesian inference.

4. **Inference for Each Object:**
   - For each TNO, compute posterior distributions over mixing weights and spectral components using the trained B-MoE model.
   - Calculate classification probabilities for each spectral class based on the posterior distributions.

5. **Probabilistic Spectral Unmixing:**
   - Deconvolve observed fluxes into individual spectral components.
   - Account for uncertainties in photometric measurements and possible inaccuracies in spectral templates.
   - Combine photometric data with spectral templates to reconstruct the object's spectral energy distribution.

6. **Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA):**
   - Apply LDA to identify latent spectral themes in the sparse data.
   - Discover unsupervised spectral classes by analyzing the underlying structure of the spectral data.

7. **Active Learning for Survey Optimization:**
   - Select the next TNO for follow-up spectroscopy based on current posterior probabilities.
   - Focus on objects with highest uncertainty or lowest probabilities to maximize information gain.

8. **Outlier Detection:**
   - Use posterior probabilities across spectral classes to identify objects with significantly lower likelihoods.
   - Flag such objects as potential outliers or misclassifications.

9. **Output:**
   - Reconstructed spectral energy distributions for each TNO.
   - Classification probabilities for each spectral class.
   - Outlier flags for anomalous objects.

10. **End.**

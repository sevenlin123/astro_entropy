**Pseudocode for the Core Computational Algorithm**

1. **Initialize Parameters**  
   - Set initial values for the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) parameters, including means, covariances, and mixing coefficients for each component.  
   - Initialize the wavelet transform parameters and any other necessary hyperparameters.  

2. **Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)**  
   - Apply the DWT to the observed photometric flux measurements to obtain wavelet coefficients across multiple resolution levels.  
   - This step aims to denoise the data by capturing local spectral features at different scales.  

3. **Training the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)**  
   - Train the GMM on the wavelet coefficients obtained from the DWT.  
   - The GMM will capture the underlying spectral variability, with each component representing a different spectral type.  

4. **Expectation-Maximization (EM) Algorithm**  
   - **E-step**: Compute the posterior probabilities of the spectral components given the current model parameters.  
   - **M-step**: Update the model parameters (means, covariances, mixing coefficients) to maximize the likelihood of the observed data.  
   - Repeat the E and M steps until convergence or a predefined number of iterations.  

5. **Inference of Spectral Parameters**  
   - Use the trained GMM and the EM algorithm to infer the most likely spectral parameters.  
   - Incorporate uncertainties from the photometric measurements and the denoised wavelet coefficients.  

6. **Reconstruct Probable Spectral Energy Distributions (SEDs)**  
   - Using the inferred spectral parameters, reconstruct the probable SEDs of the trans-Neptunian objects.  
   - Output the reconstructed SEDs as the final result.  

**End of Algorithm**

Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
    - Training Spectra: A set of reference TNO spectra
    - Photometric Observations: Sparse multi-band photometry measurements
    - Filter Set: NIRCam filter transmission curves

Output:
    - Reconstructed Spectrum: High-resolution spectral energy distribution
    - Latent Coefficients: PCA-derived coefficients
    - Classification Probabilities: Taxonomic group probabilities

Steps:

1. Preprocessing:
    a. Normalize and preprocess the training spectra.
    b. Select relevant wavelength ranges and features.

2. Principal Component Analysis (PCA):
    a. Perform PCA on the training spectra to extract principal components.
    b. Determine the optimal number of principal components (K) via validation.
    c. Construct the PCA basis matrix for dimensionality reduction.

3. Data Augmentation:
    a. Generate synthetic latent vectors using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) on the PCA coefficients of the training spectra.
    b. Smooth the latent space with a Gaussian kernel to create a continuous manifold.

4. Forward Modeling:
    a. Simulate photometric observations for each augmented spectrum by convolving with the filter set.
    b. Inject Gaussian noise (20% relative uncertainty) into the synthetic photometry to simulate real-world conditions.

5. Training theRegressor:
    a. Use AutoML (e.g., XGBoost) to train a regressor mapping synthetic photometry to latent coefficients.
    b. Include noise in training to ensure robustness to photometric uncertainties.

6. Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV):
    a. For each held-out spectrum:
        i. Remove the held-out spectrum from the training set.
        ii. Retrain the regressor using the remaining training data.
        iii. Generate synthetic photometry for evaluation.
        iv. Predict latent coefficients for the held-out photometry.
        v. Perform inverse PCA transform to reconstruct the spectrum.

7. Posterior Sampling:
    a. Use Gaussian Copula to sample the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients.
    b. Ensure the reconstructed spectra respect physical constraints and dependencies.

8. Spectral Reconstruction:
    a. Project the predicted latent coefficients through the inverse PCA transform to obtain the full spectrum.
    b. Validate reconstruction accuracy using metrics like RMSE and credible interval coverage.

9. Taxonomy Classification:
    a. Train a secondary classifier on the PCA coefficients to predict taxonomic probabilities.
    b. Classify the held-out spectra based on the predicted latent means.

10. Applications:
    a. Optimize filter configurations for NIRCam by evaluating information content.
    b. Detect outliers by assessing the probability of photometric observations in the latent space.

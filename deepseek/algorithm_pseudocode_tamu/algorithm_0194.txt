### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

**Inputs:**  
- Observed photometric magnitudes $\mathbf{m}$ for a trans-Neptunian object (TNO).  
- Training set of reference TNO spectra $\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}}$ and their corresponding photometric measurements.  
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA) decomposition of $\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}}$ into principal components (PCs) and latent coefficients.  
- Synthetic latent samples generated via Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) augmentation.  

**Outputs:**  
- Reconstructed high-resolution spectrum $S_{\text{reconstructed}}$.  
- Posterior probabilities over spectral taxonomic classes.  
- Uncertainty estimates on the reconstructed spectrum.  

---

#### **Step 1: Define the Latent Space**  
1. Perform PCA on the reference TNO spectra $\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}}$ to obtain principal components $\mathbf{U}$ and latent coefficients $\mathbf{z}_{\text{train}}$.  
2. Augment the latent space using KDE to generate synthetic samples $\mathbf{z}_{\text{synthetic}}$ that smooth the distribution of $\mathbf{z}_{\text{train}}$.  

---

#### **Step 2: Forward Modeling**  
1. Simulate synthetic photometry $\mathbf{m}_{\text{synthetic}}$ for each synthetic latent sample $\mathbf{z}_{\text{synthetic}}$ by convolving with the instrumental response (e.g., JWST/NIRCam filters).  
2. Inject Gaussian noise with a specified relative uncertainty (e.g., 20%) into $\mathbf{m}_{\text{synthetic}}$ to mimic real photometric measurements.  

---

#### **Step 3: Train the AutoML Regressor**  
1. Combine the real training photometry $\mathbf{m}_{\text{train}}$ (excluding the held-out object) and synthetic photometry $\mathbf{m}_{\text{synthetic}}$ into a unified training dataset.  
2. Use AutoML (e.g., AutoGluon) to train a regressor $\mathcal{M}$ that maps photometric inputs $\mathbf{m}$ to latent coefficients $\mathbf{z}$.  
3. Validate the regressor using leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) to ensure unbiased evaluation.  

---

#### **Step 4: Inference and Reconstruction**  
1. For the observed photometry $\mathbf{m}$ of the target TNO:  
   - Use the trained regressor $\mathcal{M}$ to predict the latent coefficients $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$.  
2. Apply the inverse PCA transform to $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ to reconstruct the high-resolution spectrum $S_{\text{reconstructed}}$.  

---

#### **Step 5: Posterior Sampling and Uncertainty Quantification**  
1. Sample the joint posterior distribution of $\mathbf{z}$ using a Gaussian Copula framework to preserve physical correlations in the latent space.  
2. Generate an ensemble of reconstructed spectra $S_{\text{reconstructed}}^{(i)}$ for $i = 1, ..., N_{\text{samples}}$.  
3. Compute empirical credible intervals for the reconstructed spectrum based on the ensemble.  

---

#### **Step 6: Taxonomic Classification**  
1. Project the predicted latent coefficients $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ into a pre-trained taxonomic classifier.  
2. Obtain posterior probabilities over spectral taxonomic classes (e.g., Water-type, CO$_2$-type, Organics-type).  

---

### Pseudocode Representation:

```python
function spectral_reconstruction(photometry m):
    # Step 1: Define the latent space
    pca_model = train_pca(reference_spectra)
    z_train = pca_model.transform(reference_spectra)
    z_synthetic = generate_kde_samples(z_train)

    # Step 2: Forward modeling
    m_synthetic = forward_model(z_synthetic, instrumental_response)
    m_synthetic = add_noise(m_synthetic, noise_level=0.2)

    # Step 3: Train the AutoML regressor
    training_data = combine(real_photometry_excluding_held_out, m_synthetic)
    regressor = auto_ml_train(training_data, target=z_synthetic)

    # Step 4: Inference and reconstruction
    z_hat = regressor.predict(m)
    s_reconstructed = pca_model.inverse_transform(z_hat)

    # Step 5: Posterior sampling and uncertainty quantification
    z_samples = gaussian_copula_sample(z_hat)
    s_samples = [pca_model.inverse_transform(z) for z in z_samples]
    compute_credible_intervals(s_samples)

    # Step 6: Taxonomic classification
    taxonomic_probs = taxonomic_classifier.predict_proba(z_hat)

    return s_reconstructed, taxonomic_probs,credible_intervals
```

---

**Notes:**  
- The PCA decomposition and the number of components (K) are determined during model training and validation (e.g., K=8–10 for optimal results).  
- The synthetic data augmentation ensures numerical stability and generalization in low-sample regimes.  
- Gaussian Copula sampling preserves physical correlations in the latent space, ensuring realistic uncertainty quantification.

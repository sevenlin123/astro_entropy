Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
    - Sparse photometric measurements m (vector of observed photometry)
    - Training set of reference spectra S_train (matrix of known spectra)
    - Filter response functions of the observation instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam)

Output:
    - Reconstructed spectrum S (high-resolution spectrum)
    - Posterior distribution over latent coefficients z
    - Probabilistic taxonomic classification

Steps:

1. Latent Space Definition:
    a. Perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on S_train to obtain principal components U, variance explained vector Λ, and mean μ.
    b. Project S_train onto U to get latent coefficients z_train = (S_train - μ) * U^T.

2. Data Augmentation:
    a. Apply Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) with Gaussian kernels on z_train to generate synthetic latent coefficients z_synthetic.
    b. Smooth the latent space using bandwidth h ~ 0.5 to ensure physical plausibility.

3. Forward Modeling:
    a. Simulate synthetic photometry m_synthetic for each z in z_synthetic by convolving with filter responses and adding Gaussian noise (σ = 20%).

4. Training:
    a. Train an AutoML regressor (ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees) to map m to latent coefficients z.
    b. Retain K components (optimized K ≈ 8-10) to balance reconstruction fidelity and numerical stability.

5. Inference:
    a. Predict latent coefficients z_posterior from input photometry m using the trained regressor.
    b. Sample from z_posterior using Gaussian Copula to preserve latent space correlations.
    c. Project z_posterior through inverse PCA to reconstruct spectrum S = μ + z * U + noise.

6. Classification:
    a. Classify the latent coefficients into taxonomic groups (Water, CO2, Organic, Methanol) using a trained classifier.
    b. Output the probability distribution over spectral types.

7. Uncertainty Quantification:
    a. Compute credible intervals for S based on the posterior distribution.
    b. Validate coverage probability across wavelength bands.

8. Applications:
    a. Optimize photometric filters for future surveys by evaluating information content.
    b. Detect outliers by identifying posterior distributions distant from the latent manifold core.

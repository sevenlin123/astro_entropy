### Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

```python
Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Inputs:
    - Training Spectra: Set of reference TNO spectra
    - Photometric Observations: Sparse multi-band photometry of target objects
    - Instrument Filters: Effective throughput curves of the observation instrument (e.g., JWST/NIRCam)

Outputs:
    - Reconstructed Spectrum: Full high-resolution spectrum of the target object
    - Uncertainty Estimates: Confidence intervals for the reconstructed spectrum
    - Taxonomic Classification: Probabilistic assignment to spectral types (e.g., H2O, CO2, Organic)

Steps:

1. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
    a. Perform PCA on the training spectra to derive principal components.
    b. Select optimal number of principal components (typically 4–10) based on explained variance and reconstruction fidelity.

2. **Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)**
    a. Train a non-parametric KDE on the latent coefficients derived from PCA.
    b. Use the KDE to generate synthetic latent vectors, expanding the training dataset for model stability.

3. **Forward Modeling Synthetic Photometry**
    a. Convolve each augmented spectrum with the instrument filter transmission curves to simulate photometric observations.
    b. Inject Gaussian noise (relative uncertainty ~20%) into the synthetic photometry to account for observational uncertainties.

4. **Training the AutoML Regressor**
    a. Use the synthetic photometry and corresponding latent coefficients to train an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees.
    b. The regressor learns the mapping from photometric observations to latent coefficients.

5. **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)**
    a. For each held-out spectrum:
        i. Remove the held-out spectrum from the training set.
        ii. Construct the latent manifold using the remaining spectra and synthetic data.
        iii. Forward model the held-out spectrum through the instrument filters to create a mock observation.
        iv. Pass the mock observation through the trained regressor to predict latent coefficients.
        v. Inverse transform the latent coefficients using PCA to reconstruct the spectrum.
        vi. Assess reconstruction fidelity using RMSE and credibility of confidence intervals.

6. **Reconstruction and Uncertainty Quantification**
    a. For new photometric observations:
        i. Predict latent coefficients using the trained AutoML regressor.
        ii. Apply inverse PCA transform to obtain the full spectrum.
        iii. Sample the joint posterior distribution of latent coefficients using Gaussian Copula to generate uncertainty estimates.

7. **Taxonomic Classification**
    a. Project the predicted latent coefficients into a pre-trained classifier.
    b. Obtain probabilistic taxonomic assignments for the target object.

8. **Survey Optimization and Outlier Detection**
    a. Evaluate the information content of different filter configurations based on reconstruction fidelity and class separability.
    b. Detect outliers by analyzing deviations from the learned latent manifold.

End
```

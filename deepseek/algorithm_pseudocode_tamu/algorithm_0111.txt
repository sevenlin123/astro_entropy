Algorithm: Probabilistic Spectral Reconstruction from Sparse Photometry

Input:
- Photometric measurements (sparse) of a TNO
- Pre-trained PCA basis derived from reference spectra
- Training data including known spectral types and their photometric equivalents
- Instrument-specific filter characteristics (e.g., JWST/NIRCam)

Output:
- Reconstructed full spectrum with uncertainty estimates
- Probabilistic taxonomic classification

1. Initialize:
   a. Load pre-trained PCA basis and associated latent space parameters.
   b. Define the kernel density estimation (KDE) parameters for latent space smoothing.

2. Forward Modeling:
   a. Simulate synthetic photometry for each spectrum in the training set using the instrument's filter characteristics.
   b. Inject Gaussian noise (relative reflectance uncertainty of 20%) into the synthetic photometry to simulate real-world observational uncertainty.

3. Training:
   a. Use the synthetic photometry and corresponding true spectra to train an AutoML regressor (e.g., Gradient Boosted Decision Trees).
   b. Augment the training data with synthetic latent vectors generated via KDE to fill gaps in the latent space.

4. Inference:
   a. For a new photometric observation:
      i. Pass the photometric measurements through the trained AutoML regressor to predict latent coefficients.
      ii. Apply the inverse PCA transform to these coefficients to reconstruct the full spectrum.
   b. Sample the joint posterior distribution of the latent coefficients using a Gaussian Copula to generate an ensemble of reconstructions.
   c. Calculate credible intervals for the reconstructed spectrum based on the ensemble.

5. Taxonomic Classification:
   a. Use the latent coefficients from the AutoML regressor to predict the taxonomic classification (e.g., H2O, CO2, Organic, Methanol).
   b. Compute classification probabilities based on the distribution of training spectra in the latent space.

6. Validation (Omitted as per instructions):
   a. Perform leave-one-out cross-validation to assess reconstruction fidelity and uncertainty calibration.

7. Output:
   a. Return the reconstructed spectrum with credible intervals.
   b. Return the taxonomic classification with probabilities.
